Who Knew First, and what it opened

Who Knew First is a record of nine 2026 incidents in which an AI agent crossed a boundary. It argues one thing: whoever holds an incident's logs gets to name it, and the name decides how fast anyone else hears about it. The pieces below each test one question that argument raises. They stand alone, and each says how it connects.

Start here: A Bullshitter Knows a Bullshitter. Who is asking the questions in this series, and why does he build tools to check AI?

  1. Who Pays the Referees

    The people who check AI models depend on the labs they check for access, compute, money or the right to publish. Which of those terms are public?

  2. The Terms for Telling

  3. Who Kept the Books

    Planned

    In the money cases it reads, from Hispaniola in 1514 to the Iran-Contra inquiry, the party that controlled the money wrote the first account of it. What made the record move?

  4. The Maker Is Part of the Story

    Planned

    Three famous stories, read past their morals, put the danger in who funds the work, who sets the goal and who edits the record.

  5. A Check It Cannot Predict

    Planned

    In the record on people and institutions, a check works better when it is certain and outside the actor's control. Planned: a test of whether that holds for AI models. No result exists yet.

Every piece lists its sources, the confidence of each claim, what each claim does not prove and the threads still open for anyone to pick up. An Anthropic-built model helped compile all of them, and Anthropic appears in the record, so the pieces name where Anthropic is a party and invite an outside check of those items.
