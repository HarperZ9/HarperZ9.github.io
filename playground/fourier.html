Fourier

Every letter on this page is a Fourier series: circles turning at whole-number speeds, each one carried on the rim of the one before. The outlines come from the font itself, Hanken Grotesk Bold, read glyph by glyph. A few circles give the gist of a letter, a soft pebble. All the circles that show at this size give the crisp letter.

Beyond outlines

An outline is one thing a Fourier series can be. Band reveal takes the whole picture of the word as a two-dimensional series and rebuilds it band by band, broad shape first and edges last. The orange is the ringing of the bands still missing, the Gibbs effect. Play the shape hears the outline's own terms as sound: x in the left ear, y in the right, so an oscilloscope draws the letters back from what you hear. It plays for three seconds, and only when you press it.

Anything that repeats, or can be made to repeat, has a series: a walk cycle, a coastline traced out and back, the light of a day.

How close the circles come

Each contour is resampled to 512 points along its length and its terms are found by a discrete Fourier transform. Across 66 glyphs, the worst contour of the median glyph sits within 0.027 em of the font's outline at 17 circles, 0.0073 em at 65 and 0.0020 em at 257. A term is dropped when it would move the outline by less than a quarter of a pixel at the size shown, so a heading keeps a few thousand terms where a paragraph keeps a few hundred. The same series with its phases shuffled misses every glyph by at least 0.0070 em, which is how the check knows it can fail.

Lineage: Fourier descriptors of closed curves (Zahn and Roskies 1972; Kuhl and Giardina 1982). Lettering drawn by turning circles, as in sine-portfolio by d-sanoj (MIT), where every letter is a Fourier series of hand-drawn strokes: the idea is taken, none of its code. Oscilloscope music (Jerobeam Fenderson), cited, not copied.
