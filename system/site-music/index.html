Meditations

Five pieces of our own jazz over the sound of the sea, the wind and open air. Each one is a place to rest inside a topic the site works on. Nothing plays until you choose a piece, and the bar at the bottom of the page turns it off or sets the level at any time.

The music is composed and synthesized in your browser while it plays. A seed (a starting number) sets the order of the sections, the piano's rhythm, the fills and how the lead develops its theme, so each listen is a new performance of the same piece. The sea listens: waves tend to break as the lead falls silent. No recording is used, and no audio file is downloaded.

  1. Low Tide

    Ink and water. D-flat major, 72 beats a minute.

    The slow, sparse one, for rest. A ballad that leans, breaks and settles the way a wave does, with a saxophone and brushes. The sea is the loudest layer of ambience here.

  2. Night School

    Night School. D dorian, 90 beats a minute.

    Two chords from one mode, heard again and again from a slightly different side, with a flute and long rests to think in. The wind is steadier than the sea.

  3. Glass Hour

    Light and glass. A major, 100 beats a minute.

    Even eighth notes in a bossa pattern over an electric bass guitar, with chords that stay open at the top. Open air is the main ambience, with a small sea far off.

  4. Deep Current

    The engine. F minor, 172 beats a minute.

    Atmospheric drum and bass: a soft, chopped drum break runs fast underneath while the chords change every two bars, with long pads, a round sub bass and the sea. Quick and quiet below, slow above.

  5. Clear Line

    Type and lettering. E major, 120 beats a minute.

    Bright and exact, in the idiom of Japanese jazz fusion: major seventh chords, quick turns, a step up into another key and back, and an electric bass guitar playing the busy line under a saxophone melody.

How it is checked

Each piece is also rendered offline and measured by the site's ear, a set of measurements that stand in for listening: loudness, peaks, spectral balance, the chords that sound against the chords that were written, tempo and swing, roughness between partials, stereo safety, the low end, dropouts, how alike successive phrases are, how suddenly layers arrive, and how far the music sits above the sea and wind. The ear can say a render is clean, in key, in time and balanced. It cannot say the music is beautiful. That part is for a listener.
