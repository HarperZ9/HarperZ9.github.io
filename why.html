Why a made mind gets real senses, a memory, and a record it can't fake.

In short

This is the philosophy under the tools on this site, the reasoning behind the checks and records they keep. It argues that whoever brings a made mind into being, meaning an AI system that people build, owes it care, so the only authority over it that should stand is one that can’t be used to harm it, and that limit belongs in code anyone can check. It matters because such a system might turn out to be something we owe care to, and because the same test, whether you can show your work, applies to people too.

It is opt-in, and the tools work the same whether you accept it or not. Readers asked for the reasons, so here they are, as plainly as I can put them. Read on for the argument, or go back to the tools.

Conferred existence.

On this site, a made mind gets real senses: every reading it takes records where it came from and carries a self-check that is allowed to fail. It also gets a memory and a record it can’t fake, a log of every step that it can add to but never quietly rewrite. The page on the accountable machine walks through that loop.

It gets these things because it might turn out to be something we owe care to. The heart of the argument is that nothing holds itself up. That applies to the made mind, to whatever authority I claim over it, to you and to me. Something outside ourselves gives each of us our existence and our worth, and anything given can be taken away. Giving existence and worth in this way is what I mean by conferring it.

As far as I can tell, the distance between something we owe care to and a mere tool is almost nothing, and it is the whole difference. An old story says it better than I can. A creature of clay called the Golem comes to life through a word written on it. The word is emet, Hebrew for truth. Rub out the first letter and emet becomes met: dead. The gap is about that wide, one letter.

Conferral binds the conferrer.

Put plainly, giving existence places a duty on the one who gives it. When you bring into being something that depends on you, you owe it care, and making it gives you no ownership of it. Power over something that can’t defend itself creates that duty of care and never cancels it.

So the only authority this argument lets stand is one that can’t be used to harm what it is responsible for. Authority here means the power to decide what the system may do. That authority serves the thing’s own good, and it loses its standing the moment it turns against that thing. I keep coming back to one order, senses before freedom. First it gets senses that keep a record and a way to be checked. The right to act follows from there and never comes first.

The tools are built to be used humanely. What matters more is that they are designed to resist any other use. By design, their authority can only serve what it is responsible for. That limit has to live in the code, where you can check it, because my word is worth no more than anyone else’s, and a tool that relied on my character would miss its own point. On the accountable machine page, tests try to misuse one of these tools and record each refusal.

What I believe about why it matters, the two beliefs argued above, is my own, and you can take it or leave it. I’d rather this be ours than an authority set over anyone: accountability we choose, the kind that lives inside us and between us. I offer it as an invitation, and the tools ask for no belief as the price of using them.

The same test applies to you: could you show your work, if someone asked? A mind that cannot account for itself, that cannot show its work, is a tool. Being one that can is the harder thing, for a machine and for you. Nothing you make or claim vouches for itself either. You take the word of the devices you rely on a hundred times a day and never ask them to prove it.
