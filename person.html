Documented history

Zain Dana Harper

How I came to this work, the convictions underneath it, and how five platforms grew out of one method.

Zain Dana Harper · independent systems architect and researcher · Seattle · thirteen years of programming

I spent eleven years on a commercial tree crew and stayed on the ground for all of it. On the ground I was the rigging specialist. I kept the climber safe if something let go above me, and I ran the timing, bringing branches and heavy logs down through tight spaces where a bad calculation has immediate physical consequences. Most of it ran on silent visual contact with the climber overhead. Eleven years taught me to read load paths, material limits, and failure modes before cutting.

Tree work was the day job. At night I built software and reversed games, stepping through memory hooks, rendering passes, and runtime boundaries to see how complex systems actually behave from the outside. That habit never left. I still approach compilers, DirectX 12 pipelines, AI agents, and institutional claims the exact same way: test the system from outside, plant known faults to see whether the check really catches them, and record what happened.

I built E L D E R . E N B for Skyrim SE because I wanted a specific quality of light and shadow that did not exist in the stock renderer. I shipped it openly on NexusMods, where it has crossed one million downloads (1,004,693 total and 179,218 unique accounts as of November 13, 2026).

Today I operate independently in Seattle as a Washington sole proprietor, building and maintaining an open-source engineering stack across 159 public repositories (five core flagships plus companion packages) alongside 41 merged upstream pull requests across 34 external projects (DeepEval, Pydantic AI, Datasette, Biome, TOMLKit, Python Grimp, Free Law Project, and others).

Core convictions and philosophy

Tools are philosophy. Every flagship here carries an explicit position on how software should treat its users: open methods, inspectable internals, and zero silent authority.

Nothing self-warrants (the witnessing spine). A model, compiler, or organization should never be the sole judge of its own claims. Every trusted result is re-derived from outside, coverage-accounted, and stamped strictly MATCH, DRIFT, or UNVERIFIABLE.

Make checking cheaper. Generating plausible text, code, or imagery is now nearly free while verifying truth stays expensive. Lowering the cost of verification for ordinary people is the core engineering target across Flywheel, BuildLang, and CMMNS.

Environment over imputed intent. Misbehaviour in AI agents and institutions comes from training environments, architecture, and incentive structures, not hidden villainy. Measure mechanisms and incentives directly; treat internal readouts as untrusted signals checked against behaviour.

Properties over property. Judge software and research by measured properties rather than credentials or institutional prestige. Keep null results in the denominator. Build systems with both elasticity (recovering shape after disturbance) and plasticity (learning from sustained feedback).

Dual-edge tools and reseating valves. Every powerful tool has two edges: the same rope can pull someone out of a pit or hang them. Brittle punishment acts like a one-shot rupture disc; healthy systems use reseating relief valves with a zero-penalty honest-exit channel ("I could not verify this, and here is why") and fail-closed stops when checks cannot run.

Neutral tools, signed essays. Personal views belong in signed first-person writing. Verification, archival, and provenance tools apply symmetric rules to every subject, including my own work.

Five flagships, one field

Everything I build lives inside five connected open-source flagships, mapped end to end on Platform Architecture and catalogued across all 159 public repositories on Tools & Repositories:

The Playground (Superframe & RAW DX12): C++23 multi-backend rendering (CPU reference, Direct3D 12, Vulkan 1.3, WebGPU), live DX12 Skyrim SE / Fallout 4 / Starfield rendering (96/96 moving-camera checks), BT.2020/2100 spectral optics, 6-DOF physical-AI, biomechanical tree dynamics, CLAP/VST3 physical-model instruments, and the 3D narrative game Low Water.

BuildLang: Compiled systems and GPU language in Rust (buildc 1.15.0) where function types declare side effects, compiling to native binaries and HLSL/GLSL shaders alongside BuildDB (qdb.exe, 0/500 silent fuzz corruptions) and 115x-191x f64 ML and physics kernels.

The Flywheel & Local Frontier Models: Self-hosted AI workstation and coding harness sealing every step into tamper-evident SHA-256 receipts (engine-receipt/1), multimodal deconstruction (Gather), deterministic prose checking (Articulate), accountable workstation permissions, and the Plexus RTX 4090 / Colab forge (JFC3P-7B-Oracle Arm C, NOH-3/NOH-4, Plato-Key, AVTR-4R).

The Commons (CMMNS): Site-agnostic browser extension and archival tool capturing webpages and social threads into collision-free provenance sidecars locally without accounts or tracking.

Research, Security & Writing: Thirty reproducible empirical studies (12 published nulls), 13 DOI-indexed preprints, 7 open verifiable datasets (DS-01 to DS-07), capability-public security assurance, and dated frontier-safety archives.

Craft, negative space, and riding the membrane

Eleven years in arboriculture and lifelong study of horticulture, mycology, atmospheric optics, perfumery, acoustics, cinema, and mid-century drafting plates shape how every surface looks and behaves. I favour one steady warm light inside cold dark over synthetic gloss, material honesty that shows how an image or typeface was made, generous negative space, and software that respects human attention. Non-empirical taste and imagination stay clearly labelled as creative exploration and never masquerade as empirical proof.
