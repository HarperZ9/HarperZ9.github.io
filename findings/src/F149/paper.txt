# abstract
Karst, Jones and Hoeksema reported in 2023 that citations crediting forest fungal network studies with more than their results support had doubled in the past 25 years. That sentence is now part of a public dispute. We reran the claim on the table the authors deposited, under a plan hash-locked before the table was analysed. The table holds 268 study-year rates for 11 focal studies on network function and 7 on network structure, and no denominators, which we rebuilt from the paper's supplement. {c1}On the authors' negative binomial model, unsupported citations rose by a factor of 2.19 for network function (0.86 to 4.14) and 2.84 for network structure (1.18 to 6.62).{/c1} {c2}Weighted by citations, the rise was 1.77 for function (0.52 to 2.99) and 1.54 for structure (0.85 to 5.81), and neither interval excludes 1.{/c2} {c3}Inside individual focal studies the rate rose for both: 3.0 times for function (p 0.001) and 3.5 times for structure (p 0.009).{/c3} {c4}With this many focal studies, a true doubling gives an interval clear of 1 only 0.425 of the time, so an interval that includes 1 is no evidence against doubling.{/c4} "Doubled" fairly describes our refit of the authors' model; their own fitted curve was not available to us. Counted as a share of all citations the rise is smaller and less certain.

# section What this means for a reader {sec:reader}
If you repeat that misleading citations about forest fungal networks doubled, say "rose, and about doubled on the authors' model", with the interval beside it. The rise is real inside the studies that were cited. How large it is depends on whether a year with one citation counts as much as a year with thirty, and with 18 focal studies the uncertainty stays wide either way. Nothing here bears on whether the networks exist or move resources between trees.

# section Introduction and motivation {sec:intro}
A {g:cmn}common mycorrhizal network{/g} is a set of fungal threads that colonise the roots of two or more plants at once. Popular accounts have described such networks as channels through which trees share carbon and warnings. Karst, Jones and Hoeksema {cite:karst2023} reviewed the field evidence and the way it is cited. They read the later citations of 18 field studies, which we call {g:focal-study}focal studies{/g}, and coded each citation as supported or not by what the focal study found. In their abstract they report that {g:unsupported-citation}unsupported citations{/g} "have doubled in the past 25 years". An Author Correction followed {cite:karst2023corr}.

The paper drew replies. Klein and colleagues {cite:klein2023} accepted that some of its criticism is justified and argued that evidence for carbon transfer between trees through these networks is solid and growing. Simard, Ryan and Perry {cite:simard2025} replied to three of its critiques; among other points they called its rationale for counting citations by year vague. Neither reply, as far as we read them at abstract and summary level, reanalysed the deposited numbers.

The doubling figure is a statistic, and the authors deposited the table behind it under CC0. That makes it checkable at low cost. Our question was narrow: on that table, does the rate of unsupported citations double over the study period for both kinds of claim, and does the answer hold when each year is weighted by how many citations it holds? The decision the reread informs is how our own work may quote the figure. Searches run on 9 November 2026 for a numeric reanalysis of the deposit returned none. That is what the searches returned; it does not show that none exists.

# section Preregistration and design {sec:prereg}
The {g:preregistration}preregistration{/g} was hash-locked at 06:07:12 UTC on 31 October 2026, before the table was analysed. It was not blind, and it lists what had been seen: the deposit's metadata in full, the header row of the table, and one data row printed by accident while checking the header. From an earlier reading of ours we also knew the per-year numerators in the paper's supplementary Note 3 and the method in its Note 2: a negative binomial regression of the percentage on indexed year. The article body and its main figure were behind a paywall and unread, so the authors' fitted curve and their own definition of doubled were not available to us.

The plan fixed a definition. Doubled means: the ratio of the fitted rate at the end of each group's span to the fitted rate at its start has a 95 percent interval, from resampling focal studies, that excludes 1 and covers 2, and the point estimate beats year-shuffled rates. Function and structure are read separately.

The plan named five measures, set out in the methods, with a {g:verdict}verdict{/g} for each and a calibration on planted data. {g:kill-condition}Kill conditions{/g}, without discretion:
- If the calibration fails, no doubling verdict stands.
- If the authors' model fails in both groups, the fitted curve does not support doubling on this table.
- If the authors' model passes and the within-study model fails, the rise comes from a change in which studies are cited.
- If the citation-weighted model fails beside a pass of the authors' model, the rise depends on thin early years.

The plan also recorded our own forecasts, set against the outcomes in Table {ref:tab:forecast}.

# section Data and provenance {sec:data}
The data are the authors' Dataverse deposit {cite:dataverse}, released under CC0. Its table gives, for each focal study and each citing year, the {g:rate-table}study-year rate{/g} of unsupported citations. A "Count" column holds 100 times the rate, a percentage. The file holds 272 rows; 4 had a blank rate and were skipped and counted, leaving 268 rates: 158 for 11 function studies over 1997 to 2022 and 110 for 7 structure studies over 1999 to 2022. The year 2022 is incomplete.

The table holds no denominators. We built each {g:denominator-rebuild}rebuilt denominator{/g} from the per-year numerators printed in the paper's supplementary Note 3: for a nonzero rate and a known numerator {m:k}, the whole number {m:n} with rate {m:k/n}. Where no whole number fits, the reduced fraction's denominator was used as a lower bound. Years with a rate of zero carry no numerator information and shared what remained of the study's reviewed total, the weakest step in the rebuild. The supplement is copyrighted, so this paper reports only derived values from it.

# section Methods {sec:methods}
## Integrity and rebuild
Two checks came first. The integrity check required the Count column to equal 100 times the rate, the expected numbers of studies, and group means of the yearly rate within 1.5 points of the means stated by the authors, 32.2 and 14.8 percent. The rebuild check required at least 0.7 of nonzero rates to give a whole-number denominator and a median gap of at most 0.2 between rebuilt numerators and the totals in the paper's tables. A failed rebuild would void only the citation-weighted measure.

## The authors' model as deposited
Following Note 2, a {g:negative-binomial}negative binomial model{/g} with a log link was fitted to the percentage in each study-year on the indexed year {m:t}, using every row including the incomplete 2022:
$$ \ln \mu_t = a + \beta t {eq:nb}
The ratio over a group's span {m:T} (25 years for function, 23 for structure) is
$$ R = e^{\beta T} {eq:ratio}
The same fit without 2022 is reported beside it. This model gives every study-year equal weight, a year with one citation as much as a year with thirty.

## Within study
New focal studies enter the pool over time, so a pooled rise could come from which studies are cited when. The {g:within-study}within-study model{/g} gives each focal study {m:s} its own level:
$$ \ln \mu_{st} = a_s + \beta t {eq:fe}
fitted as a {g:quasi-poisson}quasi-Poisson fit{/g}. It passes if the slope is above 0 and above the 95th percentile of its year-shuffled control.

## Citation-weighted
The {g:citation-weighted}citation-weighted share model{/g} fits the rebuilt count {m:k} of unsupported citations out of {m:n} in each study-year by a binomial logistic regression on year:
$$ \ln \frac{p_t}{1 - p_t} = a + \beta t {eq:logit}
and reports the ratio of the fitted share at the last year to the share at the first:
$$ R_w = \frac{p_{end}}{p_{start}} {eq:rw}

## Intervals and reported quantities
Intervals for the ratios come from {g:study-resampling}study resampling{/g}, because citations of one focal study move together. Reported without a verdict: the pooled share in each group's first ten years against 2012 to 2021, the rate in the first five indexed years against the last five up to 2021, and growth in total citations over the last five years against the five before.

# section Controls and planted checks {sec:controls}
The year-shuffled {g:control}control{/g} permutes the rates across years inside each focal study, 1,000 times, and refits the authors' model and the within-study model. A real ratio or slope must beat the control's 95th percentile. Its p value is a {g:p-value-draws}p value from draws{/g}, so with 1,000 draws the smallest possible value is 1 in 1,001.

The {g:planted-check}planted check{/g} kept the real studies, years and rebuilt denominators and drew new counts from a known truth: a study effect (lognormal, standard deviation 0.4) and a true ratio of 1 or 2 over the span. It ran 60 simulations per group per truth, 120 per truth in all, with 150 study resamples each. The calibration passed if a flat truth was called a rise at most 0.15 of the time, the doubled truth gave a median ratio between 1.6 and 2.4, and its interval covered the truth at least 0.8 of the time.

The authors' stated means were recovered. Row means came out at 33.2 and 15.3 percent against the stated 32.2 and 14.8, within the 1.5 point allowance. The mean of study means gave 32.3 and 14.7, which identifies the definition the authors used.

The rebuild passed: 143 of 169 nonzero rates, 0.846, gave a whole-number denominator, and the rebuilt numerators matched the paper's table totals with a median gap of 0. The per-study totals are in Appendix B.

The calibration passed. With a flat truth the pipeline called a rise 0.092 of the time. With a doubled truth the median ratio was 2.11 and the interval covered the truth 0.89 of the time. The calibration also measured {g:power}statistical power{/g}: with a true doubling, the interval excluded 1 only 0.425 of the time.

# section Discussion {sec:discussion}
The reread supports the direction of the original claim and narrows its size. On the authors' own model, "doubled" is a fair description of the fitted curve, and the within-study fits show the rise inside the cited studies, the strongest part of their case. The size depends on a choice of unit. The deposited Count column is a percentage fitted as a count, so each study-year carries equal weight. A year with one citation coded unsupported counts as much as a busy year. Weighted by citations, function rose by about 1.6 to 1.8 times and structure by 1.1 to 1.5 times.

The power figure governs how to read the failures. With 11 and 7 focal studies, even a true doubling usually fails to give an interval clear of 1. The reread therefore does not refute a doubling. It shows that the deposit cannot pin the size down closely on either weighting.

For citing practice we take one rule from this: when a rate is modelled as a count, report the citation-weighted ratio beside it. The sentence we use for the claim is "rose, about doubled on the authors' model", with the interval.

# section Limitations {sec:limits}
- The analysis was not blind. Before the lock we had seen the metadata, the header, one data row and the numerators from an earlier reading; the preregistration lists them.
- Denominators were rebuilt, not read. Years with a rate of zero share a remainder and are the least certain.
- Every rate is the original authors' coding. Their Note 2 calls the coding subjective and names an element of presentism. We did not recode any citation.
- Our definition of doubled may differ from the authors'. Their main figure and article body were not read.
- The calibration measures this pipeline on planted data shaped like the real table. It does not test the coding.
- Nothing here bears on whether common mycorrhizal networks exist or move resources between trees.
- The literature on the dispute was read at abstract and summary level.
