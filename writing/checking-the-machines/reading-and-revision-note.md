# Reading and revision note

September 20, 2026

This is a selective study of four historical works, undertaken for the revision of Zain Dana Harper's *An Open Letter on Checking the Machines*. It is not an exhaustive history or a ranking of open letters. One selection is a public manifesto, an adjacent form. No passage from these works is reproduced in the letter, and their historical circumstances are not offered as analogies for the author's position or as evidence for his technical proposal.

## Addressing a person without losing the wider question

James Baldwin's [A Letter to My Nephew](https://progressive.org/magazine/letter-nephew/) was read in the version published by *The Progressive* on December 1, 1962. That version must be distinguished from its later revision in *The Fire Next Time*. Its address remains personal as the scale of the subject widens. The addressee does not disappear when the argument turns toward the surrounding world. The relevant craft lesson is to keep a person in view across changes of scale. The new machine-checking letter returns to its explicitly hypothetical applicant, whose practical need persists after the discussion of institutions and software.

## Answering objections as part of the movement

Martin Luther King Jr.'s *Letter from Birmingham Jail*, written April 16, 1963, was studied through the [Teaching American History excerpts](https://teachingamericanhistory.org/document/letter-from-birmingham-city-jail-excerpts/), with the [Stanford King Institute's account](https://kinginstitute.stanford.edu/letter-birmingham-jail) used for the date and publication history. The full letter was not newly reviewed for this revision. In the inspected passages, an objection becomes a reason to explain the sequence and grounds of an action. That suggests a way to place the strongest objections inside an argument's movement. The revised AI letter answers the risk of misleading receipts before asking anyone to test them. It does not borrow King's imagery or imply an equivalence between his circumstances and the author's.

## Giving a conclusion something to rest on

Émile Zola's *J'Accuse...!* was examined in the [Wikisource community English translation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Translation:J'accuse...!), with the [Bibliothèque nationale de France's manuscript account](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/agenda/lecture-de-jaccuse-demile-zola) supplying archival context for the January 13, 1898 open letter. The translation has limitations and was not used for quotations. Its extended reconstruction precedes the final concentrated address. For this revision, the useful lesson concerns ordering: give a reader the evidence and the argument before the final request. Neither Zola's allegations nor his accusatory refrain is imported into the AI letter.

## Bringing a public appeal to an actionable ending

The [Russell-Einstein Manifesto](https://pugwash.org/1955/07/09/statement-manifesto/), July 9, 1955, was read in the text published by Pugwash. It is a public appeal rather than a conventional open letter. Its movement from a shared concern to a stated resolution suggested the importance of making a proposed next step legible. The new letter ends its technical case with a small, refutable experiment. It does not equate AI risks with nuclear weapons or adopt the manifesto's policy conclusions by analogy.

## The author's own voice and the revision

The original letter was read alongside the author's public essays, including *Borrowed Ground*, selected portions of *No Receipt, No Accept*, and the work on the limits of a technical witness. These supplied the controlling voice: responsibility for assisted work, an invitation to examine the evidence, and a concern for the life a person returns to after using a tool.

The revision retains continuous address and ordinary explanatory paragraphs. The incident inventory and most bibliographic qualifications move into notes. The original claim of immunity to regulatory capture is withdrawn explicitly. The proposed controls remain distinct from current implementation, and the pilot remains a proposed study. A new form does not upgrade the evidence behind the claim.
