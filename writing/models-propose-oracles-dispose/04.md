## 5. The stance

I use models all day, and none of this comes from suspicion of them. This essay was written with model help, and the compiler would be years further off without them. The rule is only about which layer a model's words may land in on their own authority. The answer is none, and it doesn't soften as models get better. A better proposer is a better proposer. Its role stays the same. Say a model got it right every single time. You'd only know that because something checked it every single time. The final call still has to belong to something the model doesn't control. Otherwise the evidence layer stops being one, and everything built on it inherits the difference without anyone saying so.

The rule doesn't exempt me either, and that's where it costs something. I'm a bullshitter by my own admission. A rule that only bound the machine would be awfully convenient for a guy like me. Elsewhere in the same set of tools, a preregistered confirmatory run is under way as I write. Preregistered means its plan and pass mark were written down before it started. I won't tell you how it's going, because no verdict has been computed yet, and a discipline that refuses a model's self-report has to refuse mine too. Whatever I publish from it comes with its receipts.

The thing I'm against is quieter than the loud fears about AI. It's the final call sliding over to the proposer, one convenience at a time, until the record is a transcript of whatever the most fluent party said about itself. My bet is we're further down that road than anybody wants to admit. Every mechanism in this essay exists to keep the no alive somewhere the proposer can't reach it. Models propose; oracles dispose. The receipts are public so somebody who is neither can check both. Go check them.
