## 5. The stance

I use models all day, and nothing here comes from suspicion of them. This essay is openly model-assisted, and the compiler would be years further away without them. The rule concerns which layer a model's words may land in on their own authority. The answer is none, and it does not soften as models improve. A better proposer is a better proposer. Its role stays the same, and the disposition still has to belong to something the model does not control, or the evidence layer stops being one and everything built on it inherits the difference without being told.

The rule does not exempt its author either, and that is where it costs me something. Elsewhere in the same set of tools, a preregistered confirmatory run is under way as I write. Preregistered means its plan and pass mark were written down before it started. I will not say how it is going, because no verdict has been computed yet, and the discipline that refuses a model's self-report refuses mine too. Any result I publish will come with its receipts.

The risk I am against is quieter than the loud fears about AI: disposition migrating to the proposer, one convenience at a time, until the record is a transcript of what the most fluent party said about itself. Every mechanism in this essay exists to keep the no alive somewhere the proposer cannot reach it. Models propose; oracles dispose. The receipts are public so that somebody who is neither can check both.
