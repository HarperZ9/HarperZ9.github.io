# Pick the Lock for Everyone

## Spoken version, roughly 29 to 39 minutes depending on cuts and pacing

*Zain Dana Harper, July 2026*

> This talk discusses suicidal depression, dissociation, substance use, violence, misogyny, shame, and harm to other people. It does not describe methods.

## The confession

[Walk out. Stop. Let the room settle.]

I have never apologized.

I have said sorry. A lot.

I have explained myself until the explanation filled the whole room. I have hated myself so loudly that somebody else had to stop being angry and start taking care of me. I have disappeared. I have blamed trauma, money, mental illness, timing, authority, capitalism, the people around me, and the general cosmic inconvenience of being alive.

Some of those explanations were true.

A true explanation can still be used like a fake passport.

At sixteen I was suicidal and depressed. I dissociated and acted out, and I swallowed nearly everything I felt until it came back out sideways. I have almost died from drugs, fights, self-criticism, and plain reckless bullshit. I have hurt people physically, mentally, and spiritually.

I cheated on tests. I made video game cheats. I lied my way around consequences. I learned where the loose board in the fence was, and I called that intelligence.

[Pause.]

I got very good at finding the gap.

In a game I would find the memory address, the spot where the game keeps your health or your score. With a rule, I went for the part nobody enforced. In a relationship, once shame got close enough, I would start looking for the exit before the other person could finish telling me what I had done.

Then I would lock myself in my room and drink and program. I would make graphics tools, modify games, and chase some strange technical question until four in the morning. In there I could build another little world where I wrote the laws and, conveniently, passed every test.

The kid in that room wanted to become what I called the Mad-Happy Scientist.

That was the dream: graphics, code, mathematics, biology, physics. I wanted to make instruments and ask stupid questions until something answered back, until I found the thing under the thing.

As a child I did not want prestige. By the time I was a teenager I chased it anyway.

Prestige was not the dream. Acceptance was.

I was low enough that recognition from almost anybody felt like proof I existed. Other people were down there too. I found real friendship, music, humor, and survival knowledge. I also found bad instruction, and damage dressed up as confidence.

I learned from Mac Dre, Andre Nickatina, RJmrLA, Kodak Black, the Allman Brothers Band, Radiohead, R.E.M., and others, because they let contradiction stay audible. They came from different worlds. I was listening for swagger beside grief, beauty beside alienation, and humor beside despair.

I thought about *Jacob's Ladder* a lot. Dali. Beksinski. Bodies that looked like shells after the person had gone missing. Landscapes that felt like the inside of grief.

*Platoon*. Bessel van der Kolk. Robert Sapolsky. Bill Nye. *Sesame Street*. Even the inhalants and experimental synthetic drugs I took as a kid.

That is not a respectable syllabus. It is the one I had. I was looking for anything that would keep pulling the thread in the direction my spirit had called since I was born.

Some peers and elders handed me a counterfeit version of being a man. Women were objects. Exploit people before they exploit you. Bully downward. Pretend not to care. Call it men being men.

I repeated enough of it to own the damage.

Culture explains part of it. Culture did not borrow my hands without permission.

The world preached one thing and rewarded its inversion. It asked for honesty right up until honesty cost status. Respect lasted until cruelty won the room. Hard work mattered unless the shortcut belonged to somebody important.

I felt wrong trying to do good and wrong doing harm. So I swung between them and called the motion survival.

Maybe bipolar is the right word for some of that. Maybe it is not. I am not diagnosing myself here. I know the motion.

That environment taught selfishness and deviance as survival. I kept practicing them after the danger moved.

Under the actions and the performance of hardness, I still believe the same beautifully lit spirit exists somewhere in each of us. That belief leaves the harm where it is. It is why I think repair is worth building for.

I took another road. Then I spent years treating that turn as a permanent sentence. Every unfinished tool became proof that I had not abandoned the dream. Every burst of energy became the start of the life I was supposed to have. When the burst collapsed, I used the wreckage to prove I had failed again.

My family and the people I love paid for that. Money. Sleep. Worry. Neglect. Unfinished promises. And the constant question of whether this week's light would become something real, or one more fire I expected them to stand beside.

So I am trying to apologize.

First to that kid.

I am sorry I abandoned you and then used your name as cover. I am sorry I turned curiosity into a weapon I could use against myself.

I am sorry to the people I love for making them carry a war I would not name.

The apology they are owed cannot happen in this talk. It has to happen without an audience and without control over the answer. It cannot assume I get forgiven because I finally found the nerve to speak.

A confession can be another shortcut.

Shortcuts are my field.

I cannot tell whether I am ethically or morally pure. I know purity is a terrible entry requirement for repair. The least I can do is take the skills I built while looking for locks and aim them at opening a door, away from finding another person to exploit.

## Black boxes

[Shift. More conversational.]

Everybody keeps talking about black-box AI, meaning AI whose inner workings nobody can read, as though opacity showed up with neural networks, the technology behind today's AI.

Come on.

People are black boxes with excellent public relations. We make a decision, feel the decision, and then hire language to defend it. We call appetite principle, fear discernment, status merit, property ethics, and avoidance self-care.

I did the same thing at home.

When I lied, I edited somebody else's picture of reality without permission. Then I benefited from what they did inside the picture I gave them. When I cheated, I changed the evidence other people used to decide who had done the work, who understood it, and who deserved trust.

I controlled the input, then acted offended when anybody questioned the output.

That is a tiny autocracy. I hated authority and wore a little crown in private.

This is why I do not trust anti-authoritarianism by itself. "No" can save a life. It can protect a worker, an artist, a child, a citizen. I also know another kind of no. It shows up when somebody else has a boundary and I cannot stand the feeling of not being sovereign.

I still lie. I still avoid. I can still turn a real wound into a license to wound somebody else. Saying so does not make me honest. It is a risk disclosure.

There is a temptation to say, "I am a liar and a cheat," and let the identity close the case. Shame loves that sentence because it sounds final. If I am rotten all the way through, nothing specific has to be repaired.

The useful sentence gives me a job:

I have lied. I have cheated. I can do both again. I need structures around the part of me that starts hunting for a shortcut when the cost can be pushed onto somebody else.

That is where a lot of my technical work came from.

Receipts, which are records of what happened. Replay, so anyone can run the thing again and see whether the result holds. Provenance, the trail each piece left on its way in. A program doing the work that cannot edit its own checker, so it cannot grade its own homework. A system allowed to say `UNVERIFIABLE` when it could not check the claim, and stop there.

My tools are existential dread in programmatic form. I got sick of how full of shit I could be, and I tried to build the other way.

Then it kind of became a thing.

## The robe

[Pause.]

Khalil Gibran gave me the image that holds the political part of this together.

In *The Broken Wings*, Gibran's 1912 novel, a bishop arranges a young woman's marriage to his nephew because her father is rich. That woman, Selma Karamy, does not lose for lack of a better argument. There is no fair argument to win. The bishop controls the standard, benefits from the decision, and sits above appeal. His robe turns power into propriety.

The robe changes shape easily.

A university can define merit, sell access to the definition, and then point to the credential as proof the definition worked. A platform can eat culture, own the route to the audience, generate substitutes for the people whose work fed it, set the price of visibility, and call itself neutral.

Creative communities can wear the robe too. A human-only practice can matter. Consent matters. Labor matters. Process can be part of the work. Pedigree can still hide inside ethics, where familiarity passes for authenticity, difficulty for virtue, and membership for merit.

I wore the robe whenever my intention outranked somebody else's experience. I wrote the rule, took the verdict, and made appeal expensive.

Hating the robe does not stop me from putting it on.

That goes for founders, rebels, artists, scientists, AI critics, anti-capitalists, and anybody else who has correctly identified a cage and therefore starts to believe they could never build the next one.

Fuck a black box. Also, fuck a glass prison.

I do not want every person forced to publish their trauma, medical history, private messages, or inner life. People need protected interiors. Power and public claims need an open surface.
