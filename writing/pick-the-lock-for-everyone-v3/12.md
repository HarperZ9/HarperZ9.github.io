We can keep arguing about which contamination is pure enough to condemn the others. Or we can examine the structures that surround all of them.

Who owns the vehicle? Who chooses the direction? Who receives the gain? Who carries the exhaust? Who can inspect the route, object to the destination and get out before the door locks?

Let go of the ethics that ask people to die proving they were pure. Keep the ethics that protect agency, consent, livelihood, relationship, accountability, ancestry, children and the earth.

Stop mistaking a route for a destination, suffering for value, legality for justice, difficulty for meaning, inheritance for destiny and the robe for morality.

Then aim the machinery somewhere useful: toward discovery, local capability, workers who share the gain, art with an honest lineage and science that keeps its receipts. Aim it toward communities that can refuse, families where a child can ask what the money means, adults who can hear a question without shrinking the person who asked it, ancestors remembered in full and recruited for neither the prosecution nor the defense, forgiveness that releases the future without rewriting the past, children who inherit our stories without inheriting every alarm, and an earth still alive enough to receive whatever future we keep bragging about.

Then picture the record without you standing beside it.

One day there may be layers of dirt, mud, stone, roots, roads, ruins, oceans, cities or materials no language has named yet, lying above whatever remains of the places where we lived. Most of us will leave no fossil anyone could recognize. The matter that became our bodies for a while will enter other arrangements.

The matter changes custody.

The impact does not wait for a body to stay intact. A future person may live inside an institution we strengthened, or escape through a door we did not lock; drink water we protected, or pay to repair what we treated as somebody else's problem; inherit a family rule we interrupted, or spend another generation mistaking it for character. They may use a tool whose author is forgotten and carry a fear nobody remembers teaching.

We will not be there to dress up the result, explain what we meant, bask in the monument, defend the omission, revise the caption, or tell the person living with the consequence that our intentions were more beautiful than the effect. The next person receives the trace.

Will you be leaving behind something you would have wanted someone to leave behind for you?

It could be a door in place of a doctrine, a handrail in place of a pedestal, a source in place of a rumor, a repaired relationship in place of a story about why repair was impossible, or a tool that can be inspected in place of a dependency disguised as freedom. It could also be a child who knows that love does not require becoming a stump, a culture that can transform without pretending it had no ancestry, an institution that can be contradicted, or an earth we do not treat as the disposable ground of our self-importance.

You will be neither the beginning of what follows nor its end. You are here for a narrow stretch of the record, long enough to receive conditions you did not choose and to change some of the conditions another person will receive, which is smaller than immortality and larger than nothing.

I do not know how to solve the human condition. I do not know much at the scale where people start pretending to have final answers. I am trying to stop turning that uncertainty into paralysis one day and authority the next.

I know the cage was built, and built things can be taken apart. I know a black box can be opened without turning a person into glass, and that a theorem can meet a kernel while a poem stays free. I know a confession is not a receipt. It can still be the first honest entry in a record that has been lying for a long time. Forgiveness deletes nothing; it is one way the record stops being a prison.

I know the Mad-Happy Scientist is still in there: tired, impatient, angry and curious, holding a book in one hand and trying to become an atlas with the other. The child at the park still opens his mouth, and this time words come out.

I am lost. I hurt people while trying to escape being lost.

I come from people who were lost, taken, abandoned, left by mothers, passed over by fathers, intoxicated, imprisoned and frightened. They were made to survive worlds that often lied about what survival should cost.

I forgive them where I can, and I forgive myself enough to continue the work. I am not asking the people I hurt for forgiveness; I am asking myself to become someone who can receive their answer.

The Earth Fair in the dream was closed. Behind the door was a factory, and the factory had taught its machines to chase the child who arrived expecting wonder.

I cannot go back into that dream and make the old fair stay. I can refuse to build another factory behind the door, help make a clearing, put down a handrail, leave the ledger open and build a surface where another person gets traction.

So get out of the mud, without pretending the mud is imaginary and without climbing onto somebody else's back. Get out, turn around and put something down for the next person. Leave the kind of trace you would have been grateful to find.

Pick the lock for everyone. Throw away the key. Let the walls rot into soil. Open the fair. Make room for something our ancestors could not protect, our parents could not name, we are only beginning to practice, and our children should not have to dream to find.

The account of the prison-gang affiliation gives context without treating the affiliation or its symbolism as harmless, and it does not demand forgiveness from anyone affected by it. The Klamath Agency Boarding School appears as part of the documented policy history in Klamath County. The essay does not claim that my stepfather's father attended that exact school. That claim would need confirming evidence.

The discussion of formerly enslaved people searching for separated relatives rests on the *Last Seen* archive, which the National Archives supports. I do not use those notices as a metaphor for my family, and the essay explicitly refuses to equate a children's book, a private family fracture and the history of slavery. The connection stays limited to recognition, name, return, kinship, and the documented ways power can attack them.

The account of sexual abuse is limited to my own experience and does not identify the other person. The essay separates explanation from excuse and forgiveness from exoneration, and it keeps unequal forms of harm distinct from one another.

The epigenetics section keeps its language narrow. Epigenetics studies chemical marks that change how genes are used without changing the genes themselves. Research supports several pathways by which effects pass between generations: prenatal conditions, parenting, culture, stress physiology and shared environments. Animal studies give stronger evidence than human studies that such marks pass through eggs and sperm. Claims that effects pass to human generations that were never directly exposed remain hard to establish, because genetic, prenatal, ecological and cultural inheritance are hard to separate. So the essay does not claim that ancestral memories are encoded in DNA.

The neurobiology section treats trauma and PTSD as variable and dependent on the stage of development at which they occur. It does not treat them as a single brain signature. Plasticity, the brain's ability to change, appears as a capacity for continued adaptation. It is no promise that every consequence reverses. The discussion of fear extinction rests on evidence that new learning about safety can inhibit or weaken an old fear while relapse stays possible. Adverse childhood experiences appear as a public-health framework. They are neither a fixed score that decides a life nor a substitute for an individual history.

The geological section uses established concepts: superposition, unconformity, lithification, metamorphism, fault displacement, body fossils and trace fossils. The metaphors have limits: people are not rocks, pressure ennobles nobody, and death does not guarantee a fossil. The claim is that material records can preserve effects, gaps and transformations after intention is no longer there to explain them.
