A learned system can begin from random initialization and train only on self-authored material, licensed collaborators, public-domain sources, synthetic simulation, or interaction inside a bounded environment. Weights are not morally cursed. The question is what formed them, under whose permission, and whether the account is honest.

Another artist may choose a foundation model and disclose it.

Fine.

Say so. Declare the ancestry and let the audience decide what it means.

I do not want a new purity hierarchy.

I want real choices and a lineage somebody can inspect.

Sampling is the analogy I keep returning to.

Sampling did not prove musicians had run out of ideas. It revealed that culture was already recursive. A drum break could leave one room, cross a decade, meet a machine, a neighborhood, another grief, and become something that did not exist before. The sampler was not the whole act. Selection mattered. Timing mattered. Chopping, arrangement, context, performance, and the person hearing a possibility inside the earlier sound mattered.

The ugliest part was not transformation itself. It was the ownership system around the masters: labels, clearance regimes, contracts, distribution choke points, and people making themselves indispensable by standing between artists and one another.

AI is rebuilding the record label around the sampler.

That is what I want to interrupt.

The model can become an instrument. A source graph can preserve lineage. Artists can declare what entered, what was licensed, what was refused, what was transformed, and who should receive credit or an agreed share. Communities can own the tools, relationships, and treasury while a steward coordinates without becoming the landlord of the culture moving through the system.

Keep the lineage.

Lose the label.

That principle applies beyond art. Every domain has its record labels: journals that confuse distribution with authority, credentials that confuse access with merit, banks that confuse coordination with ownership, cloud platforms that confuse hosting with a permanent claim on capability, and bureaucracies that turn a necessary boundary into a hereditary class of people allowed to speak.

The point is not to erase expertise, consent, attribution, privacy, or payment.

The point is to stop allowing the coordinator to own the entire reaction.

A serious creative tool could write a process manifest as it works. It could record what entered, what stayed out, which parts learned, how candidates were explored, when a human intervened, what compute was used, and what rights attached to the material. It could keep failed paths and replay the sequence that produced the final artifact.

The receipt would not certify originality.

Nothing comes from nowhere.

Human artists digest parents, teachers, enemies, neighborhoods, books, accidents, other artists, software, weather, music through a wall, and the awful sentence somebody said ten years ago that never stopped echoing.

Novelty is not the absence of ancestry.

It is a relation that did not exist before, made through an ancestry we are willing to name.

## The little pipe and the earth beneath it

The old world gatekept knowledge through pedigree.

The new one is learning to gatekeep it through an invoice.

Metered tokens. Closed weights. Shifting plans. Account bans. Cloud-only workflows. Capability poured toward corporations, governments, and the same networks that have always mistaken proximity for merit.

The surface is open as long as you can keep paying the landlord of the surface.

This is one of the places where profanity still feels precise to me:

Fuck the little pipe.

I am tired of watching people receive cognition as a subscription while a few companies decide how much capability may flow this month.

The infrastructure costs money. The models cost money. Somebody has to pay.

That is true.

It does not follow that permanent dependency is the only architecture available. I am tired of hearing *democratization* from people building permanent dependency into the product.

Still, a slogan is not architecture. I can condemn capitalism, technofeudalism, and autocracy all day and wake up inside the same billing portal tomorrow.

The work becomes concrete.

Local-first tools. Smaller models made more capable through good scaffolding, memory, routing, and verification instead of permanent dependence on the largest private cluster. Portable histories. Open protocols. Community-owned compute. Cooperative licensing. Data trusts. Labor agreements that return productivity gains to workers. The right to run offline. The right to inspect, repair, fork, and leave.

The productivity gain does not arrive with a moral owner installed.

If a week's work becomes a day and the worker receives four days back, that is liberation.

If a week's work becomes a day and the employer removes four people, that is extraction.

That is politics.

And include the earth.

There is no liberation in burning the planet to produce infinite disposable content. Data centers are physical. The cloud has a grid connection. Local computation has a material cost too. Hardware comes from mines, factories, water, energy, transport, labor, and landscapes somebody lives beside.

The planet is the ultimate external verifier.

Physics does not accept a pitch deck.

If our creative future requires invisible sacrifice zones, poisoned water, fossil expansion, and communities priced out of their own grids, we did not escape the cage.

We exported it.

So the new ethic needs compute budgets, efficiency targets, energy transparency, hardware longevity, repairability, and a preference for smaller systems when smaller systems can do the work. Local-first cannot become an excuse for every home to burn wastefully either. The point is not one sacred deployment model.

The point is that ecological cost enters the ledger instead of disappearing behind the word *cloud*.

AI can help us search materials, optimize grids, model biology, explore mathematics, and discover structures we could not see yesterday. It can also become a machine for converting the biosphere into synthetic content nobody asked to remember.

Again:

Who owns the vehicle?

Who chooses the direction?

Who receives the gain?

Who carries the exhaust?

## What I owe the Mad-Happy Scientist

I keep returning to that child because I think a lot of people have some version of him.

Maybe yours was an artist, an astronaut, a mechanic, a naturalist, a writer, a musician, a teacher, a healer, or the kid who took apart every broken object in the house because they needed to know whether failure had a shape.

Then life happened.

Some people jumped through the hoops and made it. Some tripped. Some were pushed out. Some turned left. Some found work that paid the rent and slowly separated them from the part of themselves that asked questions for no reason besides wonder.

I treated the missed road into formal research as a verdict. I let pedigree become metaphysics. The institution had a map, and I decided any territory outside it must be imaginary.

Then I resented the institution for a cage I was helping maintain.

That resentment fed my grandiosity. Every project had to prove everything. Every good week had to become an empire. Every new tool needed to repay every lost year immediately. The bipolar tendencies I recognize in myself can turn light into appetite. I find something real and want all of it: more capability, more projects, more proof, more impact, more recognition, every door open at once.

The structure outruns me.

I collapse.

People around me inherit the debris.

I have spent a lot of time trying to swallow the sun and acting surprised when it burns.

Grandiosity and shame are close relatives. One says I deserve the keys to the city. The other says I am too corrupt to enter. Both keep the world organized around me. Ordinary work starts feeling too small, which is convenient because ordinary work is where accountability usually lives.

One test that can fail.

One claim another person can inspect.

One tool that runs on a machine somebody already owns.

One artist who names the lineage.

One apology made without controlling the response.

One boundary accepted even though it hurts.

One child who does not have to manage the adult's weather.

One door left open behind you.

None of those things feel large enough when I am trying to repay an entire life in a weekend.

They are still the things that count.

I still want the keys to the city. That hunger did not disappear because I found better language for it. If somebody handed me the keys, I would probably notice the lock and wonder whether I could open it another way.

The change is that I do not want to pick the lock only for myself.

I want to open the cage, throw away the key, and let the walls rot into soil. I want somebody who never learned the password to have a surface where their work can be examined without first asking whether they belong. I want artists to have choices not limited to extraction or martyrdom. I want workers to keep some of the time the machine saves. I want people to use tools they can inspect and leave. I want a structure where the person maintaining the bridge cannot quietly claim both shores.

That sounds beautiful.

It should also make you suspicious.

People promising universal liberation have worn a lot of robes. The founder who says he is building for everyone can become another bishop faster than he can say *open source*. A movement can begin by rejecting the old gate and end by deciding disagreement itself is sabotage.

So contradict me.

Inspect the system.

Reject the doctrine.

Fork the tool.

Leave the platform.

Protect your interior.

Tell me where the argument breaks.

Nothing I build gets to inherit authority from the fact that I hate authority.


## The promise I can actually make

I used to want a killing blow against my ego.

Even that can become theater.

There is no clean terminal command for the self. The ego does not need root access, but I cannot delete it and call the deletion repair.

At sixteen I thought disappearance might solve the contradiction of being alive inside myself. Later I found slower ways to disappear: drugs, fights, code, isolation, ambition, fantasy, collapse, and the performance of hating myself so thoroughly that nobody could ask for anything more specific.

I am done using annihilation as evidence that I understand the harm.
