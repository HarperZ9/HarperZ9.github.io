Everything the film says

The training loop

The training loop. Five steps, two thousand times.

Every lesson so far has a piece of training in it. Tonight they go round together, and we watch the whole loop teach the attention model, from the first step to the last.

One lap

One lap goes like this. Take a batch of 16 rows from the training text. Run the model forward and score every next word: that is the loss.

Run backward to get the slope for every one of the model's 20,864 numbers. Then the optimiser takes one step downhill. That is one lap.

On one core of our test machine, with this lesson's own code, a lap takes about 21 milliseconds, and most of that is the backward pass: 14 of them.

The settings

The optimiser is Adam, with a little weight decay that shrinks most of the numbers slightly each step, as in AdamW. The layer norms' gains and the biases are left out.

The step size starts tiny and grows over the first 30 laps to 0.01. That is warmup.

And if the whole slope, all the numbers taken together, is ever longer than 1, it is shortened to 1 before the step. That is clipping. It fired on 76 of the 2,000 laps, 62 of them in the first 250.

Two thousand laps

Now run it. At lap zero, the held-out loss is 3.84, a blind guess.

By lap 100 it is 0.97: the easy patterns, which word tends to follow which, come first.

Will copying a word from far back be learned early, late, or bit by bit? (the film pauses here)

Copying a word from far back comes late, and then fast. At lap 500, copying is right 43 percent of the time. By lap 750, 97 percent.

We ran the loop again from the same seeds, and got the attention lesson's model back exactly: 29 of 29 weight tables, number for number.

Change one thing

Change one setting at a time, train for 1,000 laps, and compare. First, the noise: the same recipe on two other sets of training rows ends at 0.70 and 0.70, and the original at 0.70, all three within a few thousandths. A difference smaller than that cannot be told apart from luck.

A step size ten times smaller ends at 0.78, and copies right only 54 percent of the time. Ten times larger ends worse, at 0.95, copying 22 percent.

Make the step size ten times larger. Faster, or worse? (the film pauses here)

Without weight decay: 0.70, inside the noise. On this small model, at this length, it makes no difference we can see.

Batches of 4 rows for the same number of laps see a quarter of the text, and end at 0.87. Given the same text over four times the laps, they still end at 0.76.

A loop that cannot learn

Two broken loops, as checks. With a step size of zero, nothing moves: the loss stays at 3.84.

Turn every slope around, so each step goes uphill, and the loss climbs to 68. The loop only works because the slope points the right way.

What this lesson leaves out

Each changed setting is one run with one seed; only differences larger than the spread of the noise runs mean anything.

Timings are from one test machine; the lab's runs in your browser will be slower or faster.
