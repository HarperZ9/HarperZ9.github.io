## LinkedIn

I published a long essay today: No Receipt, No Accept.

It is about the asymmetry that defines this decade of AI: generating expert-shaped output became nearly free, and verifying it stayed exactly as expensive as it always was. I call that gap review debt. It lands on maintainers, reviewers, clinicians, clerks, and eventually on the shared record itself.

The essay walks through the verification architecture I have been building in the open, one claim end to end: independent checking, sealed receipts, three honest verdicts (verified, drift, unverifiable), and failure kept on purpose. Then it takes the argument to the places it actually matters: labor and who captures the productivity, safety arguments that double as moats, measurement honesty, and what open tools owe the people who use them.

About 19,400 words. AI-assisted, author-directed, disclosed inside the essay, and checkable against the public record it came from.

https://harperz9.github.io/no-receipt-no-accept.html
