## One line

Machines made claiming nearly free. Checking stayed expensive. This essay names the gap, walks through working machinery that closes it, and argues for opening that machinery to everyone.

## What it covers

- **Review debt.** Generating expert-shaped output now costs almost nothing; verifying it costs what it always did. The essay names who pays that gap and what it does to the shared record.
- **The loop.** A working verification architecture, walked end to end with a single claim: witness, context, open judgment, an independent checker that returns one of three verdicts (verified, drift, unverifiable), sealed receipts, and memory that only verified results may enter.
- **Independence as dimensions.** Different model family, different corpus, different toolchain, different institution. "Ask for the witness, not the confidence."
- **Discovery as instrument, not oracle.** What actually moved this year (search) and what did not (proof), and why the difference decides whether AI output is discovery or pollution.
- **The labor question.** When the machine makes work ten times more productive, who gets the other nine.
- **Safety as moat.** How a real safety concern gets bent into the exact enclosure the business model wanted, and what independent verification would look like instead.
- **The record eating itself.** Model collapse, provenance, and archives of verifiably human text as instrument-grade infrastructure.
- **Honest measurement.** Denominators, intervals, does-not-prove clauses, and the defense of the honest null.
- **The personal stakes, disclosed.** The author's own machine-fabricated author identity across four thousand commits, and the purge of his own inflated record, told as calibration for the argument.

## Quote

"Do not trust this because it moved you, least of all because it moved you. The parts about the machinery, you can check. The parts about the world are argued, not proven, and arguments are proposals: contradict me, fork the tools, break the reasoning in public, make me issue the correction."
