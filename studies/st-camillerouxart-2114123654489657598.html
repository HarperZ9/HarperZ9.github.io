camillerouxart, 'Drop the pigment. Let it decide.' (JavaScript + GLSL)

Post by Camille Roux - Generative artist (@camillerouxart), 2026-10-24

What it is, and who made it

Four stills of a real-time pigment simulation: coloured rivulets branch, bead and ring across a gridded paper-coloured ground.

Parts, layer by layer

  • Ground: warm paper with a faint grid; palette leads #e5d6bc, #e0d2b8 (M).
  • Streams: branching rivulets in blue, red, orange, green, with beads along them and closed rings (O).
  • Edges: granular dark rims and lighter interiors (O).
  • Measures: spectral slope β 2.64, edge softness 4.55 (M).
  • Fluid reading: viscosity, surface tension, diffusion; additive pigment; tide lines and bubbles; processes advection, growth, sedimentation, bleed.

The framework behind it

Capillary flow on paper: pigment moves along wet paths, the path branches, surface tension pinches the stream into beads, and the drying edge leaves a darker line. 'Let it decide' names the method: set the laws and the drop, not the outcome.

The same idea in distant fields

  • Viscous fingering in Hele-Shaw cells.
  • River networks and leaf venation.
  • Lichtenberg figures (branching discharge).
  • Wicking in fabric.

Dual edge

Generative pigment flows are easy to mass-produce as decoration; the value is in the laws and the setting. Credit the author's method if we show the idea publicly.
