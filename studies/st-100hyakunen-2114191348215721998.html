100hyakunen, bookshop rooms drawn as a mosaic of marks

Post by 古本屋 百年 (@100hyakunen), 2026-10-25

What it is, and who made it

Two pen drawings of a person browsing in a room of bookshelves. The shelves fill the frame; each run of books is drawn as one repeated mark. Pale-green pencil perspective guides sit under the ink.

Parts, layer by layer

  • Ground: paper; green coloured-pencil guides (O). Palette leads #aaa496, #c1beb0, #d1cec4 (M).
  • Mark vocabulary (13 types, O): plain verticals; stacked short dashes; dot columns; line-and-dot; loops and curls; scallop rows; cross-hatched blocks; solid black blocks with white gaps; horizontal dash stacks; small open squares; forked marks; zigzag diagonal hatching; hooks.
  • Rule: one mark per shelf run, so the type of book is coded by mark (O).
  • Measures: spectral slope β 1.38 and 1.65 (rough, mark-dense, M); edge softness 1.6 to 1.9 (hard pen, M); texture wavelength 0.004 to 0.006 of width (the spine pitch, M).
  • Figure: one visitor per room, in the same pen (O).

The framework behind it

A mosaic whose tiles are marks, not colours: each tile carries a texture that stands for a kind of thing. It is a legend made of drawing, the same idea as a map's hatch patterns.

The same idea in distant fields

  • Cartography and geology maps: hatch and dot patterns encode rock and land types.
  • Textiles: weave structures named by their marks.
  • Musical notation: repeated marks as rhythm.
  • Halftone: dots standing for tone, here standing for kind.

Dual edge

Marks as categories invite reading meaning where none is fixed; a legend must travel with them. The drawings are someone's work; we take the mark idea, not the drawings.
