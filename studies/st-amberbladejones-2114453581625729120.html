Post by Eyecager Sketchbook (@amberbladejones), 2026-10-25

What it is, and who made it

Four zine pages on composition for illustration: colour temperature, neutral against dynamic framing, depth planes, and horizon and mass in vertical frames.

Parts, layer by layer

  • DEPTH: foreground frames, midground holds the character and focal points, background sets the scene; "each plane has a job"; the foreground is often the smallest element and can be dropped (O).
  • Horizon and mass: where the horizon sits and how big the shapes are "make or break" a vertical composition; 50/50 splits are "a tough sell"; equal masses "feel too the same" (O).
  • Neutral and dynamic: neutral pose, camera and background connect to a character; dynamic ones build tension; the background sets focal length; mixing them carelessly causes dissonance (O).
  • COLOR TEMPERATURE, measured in CIE Lab from the page:
    • macro: sky (cool) b* −22.3; ground (warm) b* +42.4 (M);
    • micro swatches: pink-warm b* 10.4, cream 14.8, saturation band 13.4, orange cream 22.6, against the "cool" green a* −10.0, b* 11.0 (M): the cool swatch is greener, not bluer;
    • foliage: lit green a* −4.9, shadow a* +6.2 (M): the "red in the shadows" tip measured.

The framework behind it

A layer model with roles (planes that each do one job) and a property layer (temperature) that can be painted at two scales. For the fluid-medium framework: depth planes are strata, and temperature is a property field with a macro pattern and micro alternation.

The same idea in distant fields

  • Stage design: wings, actors and backdrop.
  • Atmosphere: aerial perspective cools and lightens with distance, the physics behind macro temperature.
  • Music mixing: front, middle and back of a mix, each with a frequency job.
  • Cartography: figure-ground and visual hierarchy.

Dual edge

Temperature rules are style rules from one studio tradition; applied everywhere they homogenise. Keep them as a dial, not a default. The zine is the author's own teaching material; credit it.
