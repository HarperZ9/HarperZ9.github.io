rileycx, early explorations: site design for a network product

Post by Riley Hennigh (@rileycx), 2026-10-25

What it is, and who made it

Three tall frames of a website design for a private-network product: a dark ground, flower photographs, a node-graph overlay with coordinate and IP-style labels, one orange accent, a giant grotesk headline cropped at the frame, and numbered steps where the later steps are dimmed.

Parts, layer by layer

  • Ground: two darks, page #0e0e0e and card #1a1a1a (M); ground luma 0.098 (M).
  • Hot mark: orange #fc4b07 to #f94f0c (M). Area share 0.025 in frame 1, 0.285 in frame 2 (a full orange panel), 0.004 in frame 3 (M): one orange per view, from a thin bar to a whole panel.
  • Photography: orange and white blossoms cut out on the dark ground; inside some flowers a rectangle turns grey (O).
  • Graph overlay: thin white lines and small nodes over the photos, with boxed mono labels like coordinates and addresses (O). The overlay sits over the photograph and crosses petals and ground alike.
  • Type scale: headline line runs of 87 and 51 px in a 640 px wide frame; body lines 19 to 24 px of 1080; headline to body about 4 to 1 by line height (M). The headline is cropped at both edges ('ct anythi / ere.') (O).
  • Reading order: steps 1 and 2 at p99 luma 0.965, step 3 at 0.282 (29% of white), the small label above at 0.612 (M). Later steps wait in dim until reached (I).

The framework behind it

Three layers with three jobs: a living subject (flowers), a technical skin (the network graph and its labels) and a typographic voice (one huge cropped grotesk). Colour is rationed: one orange marks where to look. Brightness is used as reading order, so the page shows where the reader is.

The same idea in distant fields

  • Botanical specimen plates with labels and leader lines.
  • Air-traffic and network operations displays.
  • Swiss posters with cropped giant type.
  • Teleprompters and karaoke: the current line lit, the next dim.

Dual edge

Dimming future steps guides reading and can also hide terms the reader should see before agreeing; never dim anything a reader must consent to. The network graph over flowers reads as calm and natural; for a security product that softness can understate risk.
