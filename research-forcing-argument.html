The Seam That Holds While It Is Crossed

What is forced at the conventional level whenever anything at all is sorted, named, or meant.

Zain Dana Harper/ July 2026/ Companion to the research program/ Download PDF

In short

Some philosophers hold that nothing stands on its own: a letter counts as local only because a clerk sorted it, and a word means something only because people use it that way. This paper asks what holds up the act of sorting itself, and argues that any sorting, while it happens, takes up a place and a moment, even the sceptic’s act of denying this; it grants that at the deepest level, when all sorting stops, nothing is left. If the subject is new to you, start with “In plain terms”; the numbered argument and its open problems follow.

Abstract

Some facts seem to resist reduction to plain descriptive facts. Facts about what ought to be are normative facts. Facts about what experience is like are phenomenal facts. The two kinds do not seem to follow from facts about how things merely are. Philosophers call this resistance the explanatory gap: normative or phenomenal facts do not reduce to purely descriptive ones. A long tradition moves the gap. It stops treating the gap as a feature of a state of the world and treats it as a feature of inference, a limit on the moves thought can make from one concept to another.

This paper moves the gap to a different place, the conferral-seam. A conferral is any act of sorting one thing from another, designating something, or meaning something. The conferral-seam is the event-form that every such act takes when it happens.

The argument for the move is disjunctive-transcendental. A transcendental argument tries to show that something must hold because thought or experience needs it as a condition. A disjunctive argument has the form “either A or B.” Three structural features set this argument apart from the class of arguments that Barry Stroud’s 1968 objection defeats.

The first feature is where the load-bearing premise sits. It sits on the sceptic’s own enacted act of dissolving the framework, so the argument never has to establish a separate standalone fact to support it. The second feature is the kind of conclusion. The conclusion is an identity of occurrence: the seam is the conferral-event under a second description. The argument posits no second entity behind the first and claims no grounding relation. The third feature is the form. The argument runs as a disjunction, in which conferral is either occurring or has wholly ceased. Stroud’s critique targets a conditional form, and this argument never uses one. Together the three features close Stroud’s gap by removing the step it was meant to prevent.

The argument faces two hardest adversaries. One is Nāgārjuna’s śūnyatā-śūnyatā, the emptiness of emptiness. The other is Stroud’s transcendental deflation. The argument survives both on honest terms. The thin index is spatiotemporal location, a position in space and time. It is forced at the conventional level (saṃvṛti, in the Buddhist two-truths framework), and the token-reflexive route forces it without circular reasoning. The thick index is the perspectival from-here-as-this structure: the point of view from which a sorting is done as this sorting, held apart from its negation. The paper declares the thick index and defends it in argument. It never derives it. The ultimate zero (paramārtha, the ultimate level) goes to Madhyamaka without qualification.

The verdict is FORCES-CONVENTIONALLY-ONLY. The paper prints it as a header so the reader meets it first. The conventional result is a genuine, bounded contribution, and the paper states it at its honest size. The paper names the Candrakirti-absorption risk. It marks the undischarged thick index as the live viva front, the point a doctoral examination would press hardest. It concedes that the ultimate silence wins cleanly and costs nothing by way of conventional argument. It manufactures no false symmetry to soften that concession.

Keywords: transcendental argument, Stroud, Nāgārjuna, Madhyamaka, conferral, is/ought, conventional truth, token-reflexive, performative self-reference, explanatory gap

In plain terms

Picture a person at a desk sorting a pile of letters. For each letter she decides one thing: local or not local. Each decision is a small act. She treats this letter as local and as nothing else, and she drops it in a tray. Call any act of this kind a conferral. Sorting one thing from another is a conferral. So are naming and applying a rule. Meaning something and designating something count too.

Next, take a claim about the whole world: nothing stands on its own. Nothing carries its status by itself. A letter counts as local only because someone sorted it that way. A word means something only because a practice fixed its meaning. Things receive their existence from outside. They do not produce it from inside. The property of standing on one’s own has a name: aseity. The claim says that nothing has aseity.

This claim runs into a problem with itself. If nothing stands on its own, the act of sorting cannot stand on its own either. So what gives the sorting its own status? Only bad answers seem to be available. In the first, the sorting stands on its own after all, and the claim breaks. In the second, something else confers the sorting, something confers that, and the chain never ends. In the third, the whole thing dissolves into nothing, and no result is left to state.

A careful sceptic pushes the third answer. She says the sorting, pressed hard enough, dissolves to zero, and nothing remains. A real position in Buddhist philosophy, associated with Nāgārjuna, comes close to this. The paper grants it at one level. At the deepest level, when all sorting stops, nothing is left.

The paper defends this reply. While the sorting goes on, it goes on somewhere and at some moment. A sort that happens at no place and no time is no sort. It would be the bare idea of sorting with no instance of it, and an idea with no instance does not occur. So wherever sorting happens, some spot in space and time is taken up. The paper calls that spot a seam. The seam is no separate thing hiding behind the sorting. It is the sorting itself, described a second way: an event that takes up a place and a time.

Look at what the sceptic does when she says the sorting dissolves to nothing. She sorts. Her claim separates dissolved from not dissolved, and deep truth from surface truth. Her denial is an act of the very kind she says dissolves. So she cannot reach zero while she is still arguing. Arguing is sorting, and sorting takes up a spot. She reaches zero only when the sorting stops. Digging under the sorting does not reach it.

The paper limits how much this shows. It shows that the sorting takes up a place and a time. That is the thin part of the claim. It does not show a real point of view inside the act, a real “from here, as this and not that.” That is the thick part. The thin part is forced. The thick part is argued for and not proven. At the deepest level, when sorting ceases, even the thin part is empty, and the paper concedes that in full.

So the result is bounded: wherever a conferral happens, an occupied seam happens. This holds at the everyday level, for anyone who argues at all. No conferral comes from nowhere. The only road to nothing is the end of conferral. That end is the absence of the sorted world, and it is no floor lying beneath the world.

1. The Setting: A Framework That Cannot Afford a Floor

Plainly: some views deny that anything stands on its own. That denial then has to face itself, because the act of denying has to stand on something too.

A class of philosophical frameworks begins by denying aseity across the board. Aseity is self-originating, self-standing existence: existing on one’s own, with support from nothing else. On these frameworks existence is conferred. It gets put into things from outside, and nothing squeezes it out of things from within. Status, authority, normative standing and meaning all get put into things the same way. Normative standing is a thing’s place measured against what ought to be. Things do not give off any of these by themselves.

The appeal of these frameworks is real. They dissolve a recurring illusion: the thought that some item in the world’s inventory, pressed hard enough, will turn out to be the ground that needs no further ground. They also fit what the sciences have found. The sciences find layers of dependence all the way down, and no layer stands without the layer below it.

The pressure on these frameworks turns back on themselves. If nothing is self-standing, the framework’s own claim cannot be self-standing. The sharper form of the pressure concerns the conferral. If existence is conferred, the framework has to account for the conferring act itself. That act sorts this from not-this. It picks out A and excludes A’s negation. It applies a convention. The act has three options. It might be self-standing, which is a covert aseity and refutes the framework. It might itself be conferred, which raises the question of what confers the conferring and starts an infinite regress. Or it might dissolve entirely to zero and leave nothing positive to say.

This objection carries real weight. Nāgārjuna anticipated it and answered it in the Vigrahavyāvartanī, the “Dispeller of Disputes.” His reply denies that the emptiness-claim is itself a self-standing assertion. His image is a medicine that purges itself along with the illness. Madhyamaka is the “Middle Way” school of Buddhist philosophy, and its ultimate level is how things stand when all conceptual designation ceases. A no-aseity framework that takes the Madhyamaka move concedes that at the ultimate level the dissolution reaches zero, and nothing persists. The question then becomes whether the framework has anything to say short of the ultimate. Does any result hold at the conventional level, while conferral is still going on, before the silence that dissolves it?

The paper answers that something is forced at the conventional level, as the event-form of every conferral that occurs. That event-form is a non-zero occupied seam. The seam has seity and no aseity. Seity is ownerless, dependently-arisen structure. It is real while it occurs, it belongs to no self-standing owner, and it arises only in dependence on other things. The seam does not stand on its own. The argument for it is disjunctive-transcendental in form, and it carries a genuine structural innovation over the class of arguments that Stroud defeated in 1968.

2. The Argument: P1 Through C

Plainly: the argument has five steps. Any act of sorting takes up a place and a time. Denying this is itself an act of sorting. Nothing lies between sorting and having stopped. Zero comes only by stopping. So wherever sorting happens, an occupied seam happens.

2.1 P1: Conferral Is Occupation

Status: stipulated and contested. P1 is the single load-bearing premise. Its thin reading is forced by non-circular argument. Its thick reading is declared.

Any act of conferral is performed as this discrimination and not its negation, and it is indexed at a standpoint. Sorting A from not-A is such an act, and so are meaning, designating and applying a convention. To be indexed at a standpoint is to be tied to a position from which the act is done. An occurrence indexed at nothing is no occurrence at all.

The phrase “indexed at a standpoint” has two readings. The paper keeps them apart throughout.

The thin index is spatiotemporal location. It says the conferral-event is a concrete particular at a definite position in space and time. This reading has a non-circular defense, given in §4. The defense earns more than a restatement of pratītyasamutpāda, dependent origination, the Buddhist thesis that things arise only in dependence on other things. A conferral with no thin-index residue at all is incoherent at the conventional level. It would be a type occurring while no token of that type occurs. A type is a general pattern. A token is one concrete instance of it. A type present with no token is a formal contradiction at the saṃvṛti level.

The thick index is a standpoint from which sorting is performed as this discrimination and not its negation. It is the point-of-view structure, the from-here of the act. It is the directedness that marks the act as this act and holds it apart from its complement. The usual philosophical word for this directedness is intentional: the act aims at one specific thing. The thick reading is what P1’s strongest modal content would need to force the disjunction in full. Modal content is a claim about what must be the case. The paper declares the thick index. It does not derive it. The step from “located at position L” to “sorts from L as this sorting” reads point-of-view structure off a plain physical description. That step reopens Barry Stroud’s gap inside the predicate “conferral” itself [high confidence on this diagnosis; Stroud 1968, “Transcendental Arguments,” Journal of Philosophy 65: 241–256].

The argument below keeps this division. The thin index carries the load, and the paper establishes it without circular reasoning. The paper presses the thick index hard in argument and concedes that it is not discharged. The contribution is bounded to match.

2.2 P2: The Dissolution-to-Zero Is Itself a Conferral

Status: granted. P2 survives every adversary.

To assert, show, or even intend “the residue is zero” is to sort empty from non-empty. The same act sorts ultimate from conventional and dissolved from undissolved. The Vigrahavyāvartanī’s “I have no thesis” reply defuses one charge, the charge of asserting a self-standing proposition. A second charge stays live, the charge of performing a partition: an act that divides one thing off from another. Even the self-purging medicine has to be administered, and administering is sorting.

The argument’s structural innovation turns on this point. The Madhyamaka sceptic runs a prasaṅga against the view that a conferring seam persists. A prasaṅga is a reductio. It draws an unwelcome consequence out of the opponent’s own position so that the opponent will reject the position. To run one, the sceptic must select certain consequences as unwelcome, as consequences the interlocutor (the person she argues with) ought to reject. To display a consequence as unwelcome, she must evaluate: this consequence damages the interlocutor’s view by his own lights, and that one does not. She performs that evaluative sorting. It is an act of conferral, at minimum a thin-indexed one, even though it asserts nothing about the mind-independent world.

P2’s status is high. Both hardest adversaries concede that an act of dissolution is performed. The first is the Madhyamika, a follower of Madhyamaka. The second is the Stroudian deflationist, an opponent who follows Stroud and deflates the argument by denying that it reaches existence. Their dispute concerns what the act carries, that is, whether it carries an index. Its occurrence is common ground. P2 needs that concession and nothing more [high].

2.3 P3: Exhaustive Disjunction

Status: valid, conditional on P1.

Take any region. Either conferral is occurring there, which is cell C, or conferral has wholly ceased there, which is cell Z. No third cell exists. Consider the apparent third option, “conferral with a zero residue”: a sorting performed from no index while the sorting goes on. Under P1 this option is p and not-p at once, a statement together with its own denial. By P1, conferral occurring means an occurrence is tokened, an actual instance is present, and so a thin index is occupied. Zero residue means nothing is occupied. The two halves contradict each other. At the conventional level this is a formal contradiction, and more than an implausible case.

The Madhyamaka probes a further cell, C′: dependently-arisen sorting with no occupied index. C′ is contradictory only if P1’s thin-index reading holds. The whole weight of the conditional rests here. The argument inherits P1’s contestation exactly. P3 is as secure as P1, and no more.

2.4 P4: Zero Is Cessation, Not Ground

Status: granted. P4 carries load in the decisive direction, and no adversary dislodges it.

Cell Z, the total cessation of conferral, is reached only when conferral stops. Going underneath conferral never reaches it. No vantage point is both at zero and still conferring. The ultimate emptiness (paramārtha) is no deeper stratum on which the conventional world rests. It is what remains when the conventional world, all conferral included, stops operating.

This premise does a great deal of work, and nobody contests it. The Madhyamika and the Stroudian deflationist both concede it. The Madhyamika wins at paramārtha by dissolving everything, the conferring activity included. That win owes nothing to a deeper ground. The deflationist denies that physical events carry point-of-view structure. He claims no ground under the conferring. He denies that the conferring is real as conferring. Neither objector claims that zero is a stratum beneath the conventional. Both claim that the conventional does not force what the argument says it forces.

P4 turns the concession of the ultimate into a license for the conventional result. Because zero is cessation, and no ground lies there, the conventional cannot borrow zero-ness. Wherever conferral occurs, it occurs from a non-zero location. The ultimate level offers no such location. That absence refutes nothing. It marks the point where the subject matter itself ceases.

2.5 C: Conclusion

Status: forced from P1 through P4, which means forced conditional on P1.

Wherever conferral occurs, a non-zero occupied seam occurs. The seam is the form of that conferral. It is identical to the conferral-event under a second description, and no ground lies beneath that event. The only route to zero is the cessation of conferral. That cessation is the absence of the conferred world, and no foundation for it. So the dissolution of aseity cannot reach zero while any conferring goes on, including the conferring that performs the dissolution.

The forced seam has seity, ownerless dependently-arisen structure, and no aseity. It does not stand on its own. It exists while it is enacted, and only then. Between conferrals, in cessation, there is zero. No floor persists under the world.

3. The Structural Innovation: Why This Is Not Stroud-Vulnerable in the Standard Way

Plainly: Stroud’s classic objection says arguments of this kind show only that we must think X exists, and never that X does. Three features here keep that objection from finding a foothold. They do not make the conclusion unconditional.

3.1 Stroud’s Objection, Stated Fairly

Stroud’s 1968 paper [“Transcendental Arguments,” Journal of Philosophy 65: 241–256; high confidence] found a structural gap in Kant’s transcendental method as later neo-Kantians used it. Kant’s method tries to establish substantive conclusions by showing that experience requires them as conditions. The neo-Kantians are later philosophers who carried the method forward. Stroud targeted this argument form: “X is a necessary condition of experience, or thought, or discourse; therefore X exists.” His objection is that such an argument shows, at best, that we must think there is an X. It does not show that X obtains. A gap separates conceptual necessity, what we must think, from objective existence, what is the case. These arguments deliver “must think” at best. They reach “exists” only by importing verificationism, the view that whatever we cannot in principle verify is meaningless, or an equivalent assumption aimed at the sceptic. Without that assumption the argument is invalid. With it, the argument begs the question against the sceptic, because it assumes the very point in dispute.

The objection is genuine and powerful. In the forty years since, philosophers have contested it and partly answered it [Robert Stern, “Transcendental Arguments,” Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, rev. 2023, high confidence]. Its core point still stands as a real constraint: conceptual necessity does not by itself entail existence.

3.2 Feature One: The Premise Is Placed on the Opponent’s Act

Stroud’s critique targets arguments of the form “X is necessary for Y; therefore X exists.” The weak point is the step from necessary-for to exists. The existence claim needs more than the necessity claim supplies.

The present argument removes this step. It places its load-bearing premise on the enacted act of the opponent who carries out the dissolution. The existence in question, the act of partitioning, comes from no inference about concepts. It is already on the table, granted by the opponent’s own performance of the argument. The Madhyamika who asserts “the residue is zero” grants that she is asserting and sorting. The Stroudian who dissolves the conferring-event down to a physical substrate grants that his dissolving is itself an act of evaluation. P2 infers nothing about what must exist. It describes what the opponent is doing right now, which she has already conceded.

So Stroud’s objection finds no gap to work in here. The argument never infers existence from conceptual necessity. The opponent concedes the existence at the start.

3.3 Feature Two: The Conclusion Is an Identity of Occurrence

Standard transcendental arguments of the Kantian form conclude that X exists, or that X is the case. That claim concerns a second entity, the condition, over and above the datum the argument starts from, which is the experience or thought in question. A datum is the given fact at the start. Stroud’s objection enters in the space between the datum and the further entity posited as its condition.

The conclusion C has a different form. It does not posit the seam as a further entity that the conferral-event requires. The seam is that event under a second description. “A conferring-event is occurring” and “a non-zero occupied seam is occurring” describe the same occurrence. Identity relates them. No relation of grounding or necessitation is involved. To ground or necessitate is to make one thing hold in virtue of another. Identity is being the very same thing. No second thing needs its existence proved. The identity makes no deep metaphysical claim about hidden essence. A physicist makes the same move when she says a lightning-event is an electrical-discharge-event: one occurrence, described a second way, with nothing added to the world’s inventory.

So Stroud’s objection finds no gap here either. The argument claims no second entity. Its conclusion is an identity, and identities posit no new existents.

3.4 Feature Three: The Disjunctive Form

The conditional form is Stroud’s target: “X is necessary for Y,” or “without X, Y is impossible.” The disjunctive form of P3 has a different shape. The argument does not say “a seam is necessary for conferral to occur.” It says that conferral is occurring (cell C) or has wholly ceased (cell Z). Cell C is a seam occurring, by the identity of feature two. Cell Z is cessation. No third cell exists.

The conditional form invites a question: given that the conditioned thing exists, why must the necessary condition exist? The disjunctive form does not invite it. The disjunction is exhaustive by construction, because P1 rules out cell C′, conferral with zero residue, as a formal contradiction. So the question whether C’s seam “must exist” does not arise in the same way. Anyone who is conferring is already in cell C. What stays open is whether the act of conferring, already conceded, is a seam-event or a non-seam-event. Feature two’s identity claim answers that the act is one occurrence described twice. The disjunction adds that no middle ground lies between the two cells.

3.5 What the Three Features Buy and Do Not Buy

These three features set the argument apart from the general Stroud-vulnerable class. Neither hardest adversary disputes them. Under examination, the Madhyamika and the Stroudian deflationist both concede that the structural innovation is real. They contest P1, and specifically whether P1 holds in its thick reading. The argument names that contest as its live condition.

Here is what the features buy: Stroud’s 1968 objection, in its standard form, does not defeat the argument, because the gap Stroud found, between conceptual necessity and existence, appears nowhere in the argument’s chain of derivation. The features also have a limit. They do not buy unconditional force for C. C is forced conditional on P1, and P1’s thick reading is not discharged. The claim is bounded. The argument removed the step that Stroud’s gap was supposed to prevent, and it never crossed that gap.

4. The Token-Reflexive Defense: Earning the Thin Index Non-Circularly

Plainly: the paper defends the claim that a sorting takes up a place and a time without assuming it. The defense uses a general fact: any actual event is a single instance, and a single instance has to sit somewhere.

4.1 What Non-Circularity Requires Here

The circularity charge against transcendental arguments says the argument assumes its conclusion in its premises. For P1’s thin reading, the charge would be that the argument assumes conferral-events occupy positions in space and time so that it can conclude the same thing. To answer the charge, the defense must derive the thin index from premises the opponent already grants on independent grounds. Those grounds must say nothing about conferral, standpoints or discrimination. The derivation runs in four steps.

4.2 Steps One Through Four

Step one. C.S. Peirce’s type/token distinction sets the baseline. Peirce introduced it in 1906 and formulated it in the Collected Papers; this paper reports it at high confidence via Liebesman, “Types and Tokens,” Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, first published 2026. A type “does not exist; it only determines things that do exist.” A token is “a single event which happens once and whose identity is limited to that one happening… significant only as occurring just when and where it does.” A type with no token is an uninstantiated abstract pattern, and it does not occur. This is no special claim about conferral. It states the general metaphysics of occurrence, the general account of what it takes for anything to happen.

Step two. Why do two exactly similar token events count as two things at all? To settle that question is to individuate them: to fix what makes them numerically distinct. The standard accounts surveyed in the SEP entry on tropes (Maurin, “Tropes,” Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, rev. 2023; high confidence) fall into two camps. A trope is a particular instance of a property, such as this redness here, taken as a concrete particular. The general kind, redness as such, is the universal it falls under. Spatiotemporal individuation (SI) holds that exactly resembling tropes are numerically distinct if and only if they sit at non-zero distance in space and time (Campbell 1990; Lowe 1998; Schaffer 2001). Primitivist individuation (PI) holds that numerical distinctness is basic and needs no further explanation (Ehring 2011; Campbell 1990). PI does not deny that tokens occupy positions. It denies only that their positions explain their distinctness. On either account, a token at no position in space and time would be indistinguishable from an uninstantiated type. Kim’s property-exemplification view of events adds one more point (Kim 1966, 1969; cited throughout Varzi, “Events,” Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, rev. 2025; high confidence on attribution). On that view the time-constituent is a constitutive component of the event’s structure, and no label stuck on afterward.

Step three. Apply this to conferral. If a conferral occurs, if the sorting act takes place, it takes place as a token event. In Peirce’s terms, this conferral is significant as this conferral because it occurs just when and where it does. A conferral with zero positional residue would have no identity conditions to mark it off as a token. Nothing would separate it from a qualitatively identical conferral, or from the uninstantiated type “conferral in general.” Uninstantiated types do not occur. So “conferral occurring, at no position” amounts to “a type occurring while no token of that type occurs.” At the conventional level that is a formal contradiction.

Step four. The performative regress. Take the Prāsaṅgika who asserts “conferral occurs ownerlessly, with zero index residue.” She is herself performing a conferral act. She discriminates the Madhyamaka position from alternatives that posit svabhāva, a self-standing intrinsic nature. She does it at a moment, in a conversation, as this speaker. She grants that she is discriminating, so her own act concedes the antecedent, the if-part. By steps one through three, her assertion is a token event. Being this assertion, numerically the single one it is and not a qualitatively identical assertion made yesterday, requires at minimum that it occupy a position. The denier cannot perform the denial without instantiating a token, and instantiating a token requires a location. If she then denies that that denial is a located token, she performs one more token act of denial. The regress closes the loop. At the conventional level, every act of denying the thin index is itself a thin-indexed act.

4.3 Why This Is Non-Circular, for the Thin Index Only

The chain of derivation runs like this. An event occurs as a conferral-token, which anyone who argues grants. It is individuated as this token. It occupies a position in space and time. So it occupies a thin index. Each link comes from the general metaphysics of particulars. It applies to every occurring event and carries no special bias toward conferral. Neither the Peircean type/token distinction nor spatiotemporal individuation mentions conferral, standpoints or discrimination. The thin index falls out of what it is to be an occurring token, and it needs no prior assumption about what conferral is. The claim of non-circularity attaches to the thin index alone. It does not transfer to anything in the thick index.

One limit on scope matters. The performative regress is airtight against anyone who is arguing. Every interlocutor in the C/Z dialectic concedes that he confers. The forcing does not deliver a general proof that physical events, taken purely as physical, carry a conferral-index. That proof would require identifying a physical event as a conferral-token beyond the substrate dynamics at the same coordinate, meaning the underlying physical activity there. The deflationist refuses that step. The seam binds those who take part in discourse. The C/Z disjunction needs exactly that, and no more.

5. The Thick Index: Dialectical Pressure, Not Forcing

Plainly: the point-of-view part of the claim can be pressed hard on anyone who argues. It cannot be proved. At the ultimate level it is empty, like everything else.

5.1 What the Thick Index Would Require and Why It Is Not Derived

The conventional thin-index forcing establishes that conferral, as a token, occupies a position in space and time. The strongest form of the C/Z disjunction would require more. It would require the thick index: the standpoint from which sorting is performed as this discrimination and not its negation. That is the from-here point-of-view structure, the directedness that marks the act as enacted-as-this-partition.

The move from “located at position L” to “sorts from L as this sorting” reads point-of-view structure off a physical description. This reopens Stroud’s gap inside the predicate “conferral” itself. The gap does not lie between conferral and some further entity. It lies inside what it would take for the conferral to be fully determinate in its indexing. The paper derives the thick index at no level, the conventional included. Any argument that claims to derive it does one of two things. It may import entelecheia (Aristotle’s term for a thing’s realized inner actuality), Husserlian act-structure (Husserl’s analysis of the inner structure of mental acts), or perspectival determinacy treated as a “real relational property.” Śūnyatā-śūnyatā dissolves each of these at the ultimate level. Or the argument reopens Stroud’s gap in a new spot. The paper makes neither move.

A candidate who defends this argument at a viva, an oral doctoral examination, must hold this boundary firmly. The three structural innovations of §3 escape Stroud’s critique for the conclusion as stated. They do not license a conclusion stronger than P1 warrants, and P1 in its thick reading is not warranted.

5.2 The Burden-Shift and Its Honest Ceiling

The strongest dialectical move available makes no claim that the thick index is proved. It raises the cost of dissolving the thick index to zero while one is arguing. The dissolutionist who presses the dissolution claim in discourse performs an act of evaluation. He picks this-consequence-as-the-correct-characterization and excludes its alternative. That evaluative discrimination is itself a sorting act. It is no first-order assertion about the world, no plain claim that such-and-such is the case. It works as a partition of the space of philosophical options. The Mādhyamika who runs a prasaṅga selects consequences as unwelcome, as reductios the interlocutor ought to reject. To display a consequence as unwelcome, she must evaluate: this-consequence-is-damaging, and not that one, by the interlocutor’s lights. She performs that evaluative tracking from a conventional interpretive standpoint while she argues.

This shows that the context of argument presupposes the from-here structure in one sense: any participant conducting the argument must treat his own acts as directed, as this-partition-and-not-that. The boundary must hold firm here. Treating-as is not occupying. The presupposition is present as conventional performance for exactly as long as the argument runs, and no longer. It lapses the instant discourse lapses. The opponent can eliminate the thick index without pragmatic self-contradiction, that is, without contradicting what he says by the act of saying it. He has two ways. He can concede thin location and deny perspectival structure, which is the deflationist’s clean horn (one of two available options). Or he can exit to non-conceptual silence, which is the Prāsaṅgika’s silence-exit. The self-contradiction bites only on denial of the thin index. So the paper reserves “forced” for the thin result and withholds it from the thick index.

5.3 The Candrakirti-Absorption Risk Named

The present argument names one specific, non-trivial risk directly. Call it the absorption risk: the risk that the Madhyamaka position absorbs and dissolves the very tools the argument uses. Candrakīrti’s Prāsaṅgika Madhyamaka, in the Prasannapadā, pressed the vikalpa point more sharply than Nāgārjuna’s own Vigrahavyāvartanī does. The Prasannapadā is his commentary on the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā; confidence on this attribution is moderate, since no independent primary-text verification was done here. Vikalpa is conceptual construction, the mind’s activity of carving the world into concepts. On this line vikalpa is itself the mechanism of error, and the conventional-truth framework is a provisional ladder with no stable platform under it. The worry applies directly. The conventional forcing of the thin index is itself a conceptual construction. On Candrakīrti’s account the conventional-truth framework has no stable floor. It is a web of designations that lean on one another, and no argument of the token-individuation kind in steps one through four fixes any of them. That argument brings in a substantial notion of token-particularity, of what makes a token one definite particular, and a thoroughgoing Prāsaṅgika may deny it.

The objection is live, and more than formal. The available response holds at saṃvṛti. The argument needs no token-particularity in any ultimate sense. It needs only that the Prāsaṅgika context of argument conventionally individuates its own moves as discrete performances. That claim is thin and conventionally available. Candrakīrti’s objection presses further. It asks whether even this thin claim covertly smuggles a svabhāva-committed notion of particularity into the conventional framework. This paper has no worked-out response to the Prasannapadā on this point. It names the absorption risk and leaves it as a live viva front, alongside the thick index.

5.4 The Ultimate Concession: Zero without Manufactured Symmetry

The Prāsaṅgika wins at the ultimate level. Śūnyatā-śūnyatā, the claim that emptiness is itself empty, dissolves occurrence, token, position and perspectival character at the paramārtha level. Even “occurrence = tokening” and “tokening = spatiotemporal individuation” are saṃvṛti truths with no residue at the ultimate level. The thin index is empty at paramārtha. The thick index goes further than unearned there: it is dissolved, along with everything the conventional forcing uses to establish the thin index.

A temptation arises to manufacture a symmetry at the ultimate level: to claim that the occupation move “cannot be refuted there any more than it is proved there.” The paper must decline it. The picture is asymmetric. The Prāsaṅgika does not assert the dissolution as a paramārtha thesis from within discourse, and she has no need to. The zero at paramārtha is no competing claim that could win or lose an argument. It is what remains when conceptual designation ceases. She wins the ultimate through the ceasing of proliferation, the end of the mind’s spinning-out of concepts. She needs no successful counter-thesis for that win. At paramārtha the thick index has no protected standing as merely unproved. It was never earned there, and at paramārtha everything is empty, the thin index included.

The cost to the dissertation is real. For the contribution, the cost is bounded and not fatal. A conventional, discourse-scoped result is still a genuine result. It says that any act of conferral occurring in discourse instantiates a non-zero thin-indexed seam, by identity and not by grounding. The Mādhyamika gets paramārtha, and the argument gets saṃvṛti. Whether saṃvṛti is enough for the contribution depends on honesty. It is enough if the contribution is stated honestly at saṃvṛti, and the paper now states it that way.

6. The Cogito-at-the-Act and Performative Self-Reference

Plainly: the move “denying X is itself doing X” has two famous ancestors. One is Descartes’s cogito read as an act. The other is Apel and Habermas on arguments that refute themselves. This paper claims far less from the move than they did.

6.1 The Lineage

P1 and the argument’s P2 move have a recognizable lineage. The paper names it openly, and naming it makes the argument clearer than a claim of secret discovery would. The structure is performative self-reference: the act of denying X presupposes X or enacts X. It has two canonical ancestors in philosophy.

Descartes’s cogito, read as an act and not as a proposition. The traditional reading treats “cogito ergo sum” (“I think, therefore I am”) as an inference. A more defensible reading treats it as a performative, an utterance that does something by being said (Hintikka, “Cogito ergo sum: Inference or Performance?” 1962, Philosophical Review 71: 3–32; high confidence on existence and thesis). The act of doubting instantiates a doubter. The force here does not come from inference. The act of doubting, as an enactment, is itself the existence it was supposed to deny, so no step from “there must be a doubter for there to be doubting” is needed. The present argument’s P2 has the same structure, applied to the act of dissolving-to-zero. Performing a dissolution is itself an instance of the conferral it tries to dissolve.

Performative self-reference in Apel and Habermas. Karl-Otto Apel’s transcendental pragmatics (Transformation of Philosophy, 1973; Towards a Transformation of Philosophy, English 1980; moderate confidence on primary-text specifics) and Jürgen Habermas’s theory of communicative action (The Theory of Communicative Action, vol. 1, 1984; high confidence on thesis) each developed one observation. Certain philosophical positions refute themselves through the act of arguing, at a level beneath the proposition argued. Apel’s “Letztbegründung,” or ultimate grounding, argument claims that anyone who argues enacts the pragmatic presuppositions of discourse, including the sceptic who argues against them. Pragmatic presuppositions are what one must already be doing to argue at all: the participants recognize one another as able to give reasons, and they hold truth as a regulative ideal, an aim that guides inquiry without ever being fully reached. [high confidence on the claim, moderate on specific Apel citations without primary-text verification]. This comes structurally close to the present argument’s P2 move.

The difference matters. The present argument does not claim to derive oughts, or Habermasian validity claims, or anything about the semantic content of discourse, from the performative structure. The only content it derives is the thin index, spatiotemporal location. That derivation runs through the general metaphysics of token individuation and uses no pragmatic presupposition about the norms of discourse. The paper acknowledges the Apelian lineage. It disclaims the Apelian ambition outright.

6.2 Why the Cogito Comparison Does Not Overreach

The Descartes comparison informs, and it can mislead if it imports the first-person singular, the “I.” On its traditional reading the cogito secures the existence of a thinking subject. The present argument secures something else: a thin-indexed occurrence with no subject attached. It secures no subject, no perspective, nothing that could be called a who. The thin index is spatiotemporal location, available in principle to any event-metaphysics, and it is no first-personal standpoint. The seam is ownerless. It has structural seity and no aseity, and the argument says nothing about whether a subject stands behind it. Here the framework’s no-aseity commitment bears directly. The seam is forced without positing an ego, a soul, a transcendental subject (a self posited as the necessary ground of experience), or anything else the Buddhist opponent has good reason to reject. The paper cites the cogito for its performative form and leaves its first-personal content aside.

7. The Arity Gap: A Companion Result That Floats Free

Plainly: a separate argument shows that holding someone responsible is a two-place relation, which no one-place description of behavior can replace. It shares this paper’s move of refusing to drop a part the relation needs.

The mock viva committee singled out one verdict in the free-will chapter as the corpus’s best independent philosophy: the arity-gap argument against eliminativist reductions of the reactive attitudes. The record sits in the credit panel of the mock viva (its section 1) and in sections 1.3 to 1.5 of the dissertation’s free-will chapter. This paper reports the argument as a companion. It is structurally independent of the forcing argument, and it shows what form the forcing argument must take. An eliminativist reduction claims that some category should be dropped from our account of the world. The reactive attitudes are P.F. Strawson’s term for responses such as blame, resentment and gratitude.

The arity-gap argument runs as follows. The eliminativist reduces blame, resentment and the reactive attitudes in general to a forward-looking program for shaping behavior, and he invokes the phlogiston analogy. Phlogiston was a substance once posited to explain burning and later discarded. The case for abandoning phlogiston-talk is that combustion, understood as oxidation, gives a target-fixing description of what phlogiston-talk was tracking, and that description is available in the third person. A target-fixing description says, in other terms, what the old talk aimed at. The eliminativist owes the same for the reactive attitudes: a forward-looking, third-person, target-fixing description of what holding-responsible was tracking. He has none, and the reason is structural. Oxidation is a one-place property of the combusting stuff. Holding-responsible is a two-place relation, and an addressee qua addressee fills its second place: someone addressed precisely as an addressee. The arity of a relation is its number of places. A one-place target-fixing vocabulary cannot fix the target of a two-place relation. Strip out the second relatum, the second thing the relation relates, and you have not removed a phlogiston-gloss from combustion. You have eliminated one of the two relata, and you now describe a different relation.

The paper includes this argument because it has the same logical form as the forcing argument’s structural innovation. The relevant structure cannot be reduced to something of lower arity, because the lower-arity description leaves out one of the relata that constitute the phenomenon. The forcing argument claims that the seam is identical to the conferral-event under a second description, and is no grounding relation behind it. In structural terms, that claim refuses the reduction to a one-place description, the event with the standpoint-description dropped. One arity observation binds both cases: one-place reductions cannot fix the target of a two-place relation.

The arity-gap argument for the reactive attitudes is type-B-independent. It needs nothing about the hard problem of consciousness, the problem of why physical processes come with subjective experience. Type-B physicalism is one family of responses to that problem. The argument also passes the floats-free test. A solipsistic universe, one with only one inhabited standpoint, satisfies the necessity-of-locus residue in full and contains no second-personal remainder, because no one is there to address. The two-place structure (from a standpoint, addressed to a standpoint) differs from the one-place monadic residue (“a standpoint is occupied”). A monadic property is a one-place property, a property of a single thing. The arity gap is a genuine structural difference, and more than a verbal one.

The paper offers the companion result because it generalizes the forcing argument’s own form. The escape from both Stroud and the Madhyamaka adds no new entity. It refuses the reduction to a lower arity. The seam does not sit behind the conferral; it is the conferral under a second description. In the same way, the second person does not sit behind the blaming; the second person is the blaming taken as a two-place enactment. The structure appears in both places because it is the correct structure for resisting certain reductive moves.

8. The Thin/Thick Distinction and the Remainder Law’s Verdict

Plainly: a separate filter tested the paper’s biggest claim and judged it a restatement. That verdict sets the size of the grand claim and leaves it unrefuted. The narrower membrane claim passes the same filter.

The corpus’s Remainder Law put the forcing argument’s verdict, FORCES-CONVENTIONALLY-ONLY, to a further test. The Remainder Law is a post-hoc demarcation filter, a rule applied after the fact to mark off which claims count as genuine results. It is modelled on Popper’s move from methodological falsifiability to a criterion of demarcation. As first written, the Remainder Law rejected too much. Its clause (iii), “not closed by any third-person inventory,” failed Bell’s theorem under blind application and so certified almost nothing. Bell’s theorem, from physics, shows correlations that no purely local third-person inventory can reproduce, which is why the clause was too strong. The revision rewrote clause (iii) as a provability condition: P is not derivable from the separated descriptions of A and B under the governing theory’s inferential closure. Put simply, P does not follow by logic from the two descriptions taken apart. With that revision the filter returns a verdict on the forcing argument’s grand claim. The grand claim says the explanatory gap is the same irreducibility the hard problem names, forced at the seam. The verdict is REDESCRIPTION, by clause (ii). On the filter’s own clause-ii reading, the claim restates the premises in different vocabulary. The filter does not certify it as a result.

This verdict does not refute the claim. It sets the claim’s size. REDESCRIPTION is the verdict the forcing argument’s own thesis had to expect. The grand relocation claim says consciousness’s explanatory gap is forced at the conferral seam as the same gap. That claim needs the thick index, which is undischarged. It also needs a rejection of type-B physicalism that the strongest objector declines to provide for the brute-identity move. Type-B physicalism holds that consciousness is physical and grants that reasoning alone cannot show this. Its brute-identity version treats the identity of an experience with a brain state as a basic fact with no further explanation. Chalmers 2006 sets out this move and leaves it standing. The argument as stated does not touch the brute-identity type-B physicalist. This is a live, named limitation.

The Remainder Law does not return REDESCRIPTION for the membrane framing. The membrane framing covers the thin-index seam, the result that authentication is not authorization (authn is not authz), and the consequences about unmeasurability and the taxonomy of failures. These results are type-B-independent. They pass the filter’s certification of provable non-entailment, which means they are more than logical restatements of their inputs. The contribution survives.
