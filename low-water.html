Low Water

One family's line through a hundred and fifteen years of one village, where the people at the table decide who eats and who is sent away. The full-scale demonstration of the engine.

It shows abuse, persecution and genocide on screen. Two lines hold: nothing sexual with minors, and sexual violence with no sexual content.

The island

The game's world, made from one seed: an island 131 km by 98 km with eleven regions, a river system, fifteen ring towns a day's walk apart and forty-seven villages. You can walk it here, drawn live by the engine that will run the game.

The world is in progress. The land, the water, the towns, the weather and the sky stand where the rules put them, and all of it can be walked. The trees, the buildings and the ground surface are first versions. Set beside the pictures each region traces to, none looks right yet; the fog forest comes closest.

With scripts and WebGPU on, each picture opens onto the island, drawn live. The walk downloads 4.0 MB when you press, and nothing before.

W A S D move. Click, then the mouse looks. Shift runs. F flies, and Space and C rise and sink. The number keys, 0 and minus go to the eleven regions. The wheel turns the clock and T holds it. Esc lets the mouse go, and Esc again, or Back to the page, leaves the walk.

On an RTX 4090 shared with other work, the walk kept to the display's 120 frames a second in the forest and the town. Over 300 frames the median gap was 8.3 ms at 1280 x 720 and at 2560 x 1440; the worst was 42 ms at 1280 x 720 and 33 ms at 2560 x 1440. Drawn back to back, a frame of the forest took the GPU 2.2 to 3.9 ms at 1280 x 720 and 6.7 to 7.3 ms at 2560 x 1440, over two runs.

The three pictures are the walk's own canvas, drawn on an RTX 4090 at 1600 x 900 from the code and world this page loads.

How the island is made

  1. Five tectonic plates; where two meet the ground lifts, and the long north-south range stands on one such boundary.
  2. Thirty rounds of stream-power erosion against uplift, each one flooding the surface from the sea so every cell has a way downhill.
  3. Lakes in four overdeepened valleys, kettle ponds on the northern shield and pools in the marsh.
  4. Rain on a west wind, spent on the climb, so the east sits in a rain shadow; cover follows temperature, rain, slope and height.
  5. A seat for each region, ring towns where the nearest is too far, villages and lone houses, and roads as least-time routes for a walker.
  6. Landmarks placed by rule, up to the 1,200 m Tower of the monument ground.

Every step uses integer hashing and plain arithmetic, so one seed gives the same bytes on any machine. The checks: 113 river lines whose water never rises downstream, no hydrology fault, all eleven regions present, and a median road between ring towns of 5.9 hours over 29 km. The generator is written in BuildLang (superframe #120), and the walk runs on the engine's web GPU host (superframe #121).

The story

A boy who did not come home, and a parent who fails him. Famine presses from the first era; persecution grows until it is a clearance.

  1. 1 The Exchange 45 minutes
  2. 2 The Quota 85 minutes
  3. 3 Allocation 50 minutes
  4. 4 The Closure 60 minutes
  5. 5 The Clearance 80 minutes

5 hours 20 minutes across the five eras. The story reaches you through bodies, sound, touch and changed places, never through a ledger to read.

Written to the ending: 591 story nodes, every one reachable. Voices, art and the native port come next.

The fights

Fights run on raw-native's 60 Hz action timeline, and the animation decides each hit: capsules bound to the bones of the swing.

  • bit for bit the C++ timeline matches the game's JavaScript combat engine

    It loads the game's move data and ports its strike generator expression for expression, on deterministic maths: the same bits on every machine. raw-native #239, merged 8 November 2026 (ab0e692).

  • 18, 30 or 36 frames of warning before a telegraphed strike, checked as data, with the slow-then-fast shape on every tell you can parry

    Telegraph retiming drives the presentation clock while the simulation keeps its integer frames. raw-native #242, merged 9 November 2026 (7cfc64a).

Next, timing, sound, physics, effects and hit markers move onto one shared schedule, and the game's own simulation takes up the capsule contact.

Read a tell

A telegraphed strike gives you 18, 30 or 36 frames of warning, slow at first and then fast. Start one, and press Parry (or Space) as the arc closes.

With scripts on, this is a strike you can parry.

The three leads and the slow-then-fast shape are the engine's own rule. The 4-frame parry window is an example for practice, not the game's number. Sound is off until you turn it on.

The rules

The rules are written in BuildLang and compiled into a C library the C++ host links.

  • 16 of 16 checks of the host's own test pass against the BuildLang rules, as they do against the C++ module

    Records that need the story expression language are refused by name until it is ported. buildlang #158, merged 9 November 2026 (3c7cc54), first released in 1.12.0.

  • 5.09x faster: the 40-fight corpus run in parallel at 8 threads, with the same bytes as the serial run

    95% interval 4.94 to 5.37, on an i7-13700KF shared with GPU training. buildlang #136, merged 8 November 2026 (bbcf9dd), first released in 1.11.0.
