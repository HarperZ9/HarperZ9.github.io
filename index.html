    Light and glass

    • Light bends at an interface by the ratio of refractive indices; from under water the sky shows as a window of half-angle 48.6 degrees.
    • A thin layer reflects colours that interfere constructively at its thickness; the hue walks with thickness and angle.
    • Light through a medium falls off exponentially with depth, red first in water.

    Type and lettering

    • In intaglio, tone comes from line density and width under a raking light, never from flat fill; the edge exists where cuts end.
    • Erosion should follow local stroke width so thin parts of a letter survive.
    • Readability is king: contrast serves separation and the focal point before poetry.

    Mosaic

    • Tesserae are laid in courses (andamento) that follow the form's contour, so the flow of the tiles describes the shape.
    • Art deco builds from a closed motif set (fan, feather, fountain, palm, peacock, scallop, sunburst, waves, volute, clouds, chevron, aircraft fin) stepped and mirrored about an axis, with light as a material (floodlit towers, the Chrysler crown).

    Ink and water

    • A dye spreads with radius growing as the square root of time (r ~ t^0.5); a moving front grows linearly.
    • Evaporating liquid carries suspended pigment to the drying edge and leaves it there as a rim.
    • A yield-stress paint flows only where pushed past its yield value and holds ridges elsewhere.

    Sound

    • The pad dips about 6 dB under each kick (5.9 dB on 92% of kicks in Stolie): separation by ducking.
    • Keep the third thin or absent; the minor colour comes from a fifth plus a flat sixth.
    • Make the flat sixth (bVI, or b6 over the pedal) the main colour move; lead cells repeat one colour tone (5, b6 or 9).
    • The room is a dark 1 to 2 s space with one wide return; harmony low-passed under about 1 kHz with slow attacks; drums carry the only brightness.

    Data and the visualizer

    • Perceived change follows the ratio, not the difference: each added transparent layer reads as a smaller step, so a fifth layer barely reads darker (Albers, Interaction of Color, plate XX-1).
    • A shape sets its resonances, but the resonances do not always fix the shape (Kac 1966; Gordon, Webb and Wolpert 1992).
    • Overlapping transparent inks multiply their transmissions; real inks absorb outside their band, so the ideal multiply misses hue.

    The exhibit

    • Split an argument into premises, presuppositions and conclusions; read conjunctions as turns or continuations; mark the movements (chain, list, turn) so the whole can be rebuilt. The same decomposition the art studies use, applied to text.
