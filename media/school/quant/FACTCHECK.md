## Other notes

- Tiny-model limit. No spoken line gives the model's size or says the results may not carry over. `sources.about` says all of it comes from a 20,864-number model. Frantar finds larger models easier to quantise, and RTN on OPT collapses at 3 bits, while ours does not when it uses one scale per output. Suggest one sentence in the close: "All of this is one model of 20,864 numbers. Large models behave differently, so the paper results are not ours."
- Readout and the paper. GPTQ's 175B run keeps the embeddings and the output layer in FP16 (Frantar section 5, "Practical Speedups"). Our film quantises the readout, the table that costs the most here. That is worth a sentence, since it shows the papers made the same call.
- Positional tokens. Every token picks a row by index (`schemes.rows.18`, `sensitivity.rows.10`). If the fill changes its loop order, the film silently picks up another scheme or method. Suggest a fill test that asserts method, scheme and bits for each row the template names.
- Typed numbers that should be tokens: 32 bits, 256, 16, 4 (palette), 16 levels, "a handful", "about a third", and "worse" at 2 bits. Only 32 is safe typed, since it is the float32 format.
- `evidence.json`: `jacob2018`, `dettmers2022` and `frantar2023` still say "to be read by the fact-check". Their claims match the papers: Jacob section 2.1, equation 1 and one parameter set per array; Dettmers outliers in activations and the breakage of 8-bit quantisation; Frantar Algorithm 1 and calibration. The `ours-quant` claims match `facts.json`.
- Jacob and others is cited as CVPR 2018. The arXiv page lists no venue. CVPR 2018 is from memory, high confidence. The author list (Jacob, Kligys, Chen, Zhu, Tang, Howard, Adam, Kalenichenko) is confirmed from arXiv.
