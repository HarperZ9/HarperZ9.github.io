Pick the Lock for Everyone

Core talk with optional movements, roughly 27 to 36 minutes depending on cuts and pacing

Zain Dana Harper/July 22, 2026/6,637 words/AI-assisted, author-directed, publicly disclosed

Read the full long-form essay. View the chronological visual sequence. The steward-platform, parallel-infrastructure, and open-failure movements can be cut for a shorter venue. The cultural lineage, mental-health turn, and final gift coda belong to the core arc.

This talk discusses suicidal depression, dissociation, substance use, violence, misogyny, shame, and harm to other people. It does not describe methods.

[Walk out. Stop. Let the room settle.]

I have never apologized.

I have said sorry. A lot.

I have explained myself until the explanation took up the whole room. I have hated myself loudly enough that somebody else had to stop being angry and start taking care of me. I have disappeared. I have blamed trauma, money, mental illness, timing, authority, capitalism, the people around me, and the general cosmic inconvenience of being alive.

Some of those explanations were true.

A true explanation can still be used like a fake passport.

When I was sixteen I was suicidal, depressed, dissociating, acting out, and swallowing nearly everything I felt until it came back out sideways. I have almost died from drugs, fights, self-criticism, and plain reckless bullshit. I have hurt people physically, mentally, and spiritually.

I cheated on tests. I made video game cheats. I lied my way around consequences. I learned how to find the loose board in the fence, and I called that intelligence.

[Pause.]

I became very good at locating the gap.

Give me a game and I would find the memory address. Give me a rule and I would find the part nobody enforced. Give me a relationship, let shame get close enough, and I would start looking for the exit before the other person could finish telling me what I had done.

Then I would lock myself in my room. Drink. Program. Make graphics tools. Modify games. Chase a strange technical question until four in the morning. Build another little world where I wrote the laws and, conveniently, passed every test.

The kid in that room wanted to become what I called the Mad-Happy Scientist.

That was the dream. Graphics, code, mathematics, biology, physics. Make instruments. Ask stupid questions until something answers back. Find the thing under the thing.

As a child I did not want prestige. By the time I was a teenager I chased it anyway.

Prestige was not the dream. Acceptance was.

I was so low that recognition from almost anybody felt like proof I existed. I found other people down there too. Real friendship. Music. Humor. Survival knowledge. Bad instruction. Damage dressed like confidence.

I learned from Mac Dre, Andre Nickatina, RJmrLA, Kodak Black, the Allman Brothers Band, Radiohead, R.E.M., and others because they let contradiction stay audible. Different worlds. I was listening for swagger beside grief, beauty beside alienation, humor beside despair.

I thought about Jacob's Ladder a lot. Dali. Beksinski. Bodies that looked like shells after the person had gone missing. Landscapes that felt like the inside of grief.

Platoon. Bessel van der Kolk. Robert Sapolsky. Bill Nye. Sesame Street. Even the inhalants and experimental synthetic drugs I took as a kid.

That is not a respectable syllabus. It is the one I had. I was looking for anything that would keep pulling the thread in the direction my spirit had called since I was born.

Some peers and elders handed me a counterfeit version of being a man. Women were objects. Exploit before you get exploited. Bully downward. Pretend not to care. Call it men being men.

I repeated enough of it to own the damage.

Culture explains part of it. Culture did not borrow my hands without permission.

The world preached one thing and rewarded its inversion. Honesty until it costs status. Respect until cruelty earns the room. Work hard unless the shortcut belongs to somebody important.

I felt wrong trying to do good and wrong doing harm, so I swung between them and called the motion survival.

Maybe bipolar is the right word for some of that. Maybe it is not. I am not diagnosing myself here. I know the motion.

That environment taught selfishness and deviance as survival. I kept practicing after the danger moved.

Under that silhouette, under the actions and the performance of hardness, I still believe the same beautifully lit spirit exists somewhere in each of us. That does not erase the harm. It is why I think repair is worth building for.

I took another road, then spent years treating that turn as a permanent sentence. Every unfinished tool became proof that I had not really abandoned the dream. Every burst of energy became the start of the life I was supposed to have. When the burst collapsed, I used the wreckage to prove I had failed again.

My family and the people I love paid for that. Money. Sleep. Worry. Neglect. Unfinished promises. The constant question of whether the light I found this week would become something real or another fire I expected them to stand beside.

So I am trying to apologize.

First to that kid.

I am sorry I abandoned you and then used your name as cover. I am sorry I turned curiosity into a weapon I could use against myself.

I am sorry to the people I love for making them carry a war I would not name.

And this talk is not the apology they are owed. That has to happen without an audience, without control over the answer, and without the assumption that I get forgiven because I finally found the nerve to speak.

A confession can be another shortcut.

Shortcuts are my field.

I do not know whether I am ethically or morally pure. I know purity is a terrible entry requirement for repair. The least I can do is turn the skills I developed while looking for locks toward opening a door instead of finding another person to exploit.

[Shift. More conversational.]

Everybody keeps talking about black-box AI as though opacity showed up with neural networks.

Come on.

People are black boxes with excellent public relations. We make a decision, feel the decision, and then hire language to defend it. We call appetite principle. We call fear discernment. We call status merit. We call property ethics. We call avoidance self-care.

I did the same thing at home.

When I lied, I edited somebody else's picture of reality without permission and then benefited from what they did inside the picture I gave them. When I cheated, I changed the evidence other people used to decide who had done the work, who understood it, and who deserved trust.

I controlled the input, then acted offended when anybody questioned the output.

That is a tiny autocracy. I hated authority and wore a little crown in private.

This is why I do not trust anti-authoritarianism by itself. "No" can save a life. It can protect a worker, an artist, a child, a citizen. I also know another kind of no. The one that appears when somebody else has a boundary and I cannot stand the feeling of not being sovereign.

I still lie. I still avoid. I can still turn a real wound into a license to wound somebody else. Saying that does not make me honest. It is a risk disclosure.

There is a temptation to say, "I am a liar and a cheat," and let the identity close the case. Shame loves that because it sounds final. If I am rotten all the way through, nothing specific has to be repaired.

The useful sentence leaves me with a job:

I have lied. I have cheated. I can do both again. I need structures around the part of me that starts hunting for a shortcut when the cost can be pushed onto somebody else.

That is where a lot of my technical work came from.

Receipts. Replay. Provenance. A worker that cannot edit its own verifier. A system allowed to say UNVERIFIABLE and stop there.

My tools are existential dread in programmatic form. I got sick of how full of shit I could be and tried to build in the opposite direction.

Then it kind of became a thing.

[Pause.]

Khalil Gibran gave me the image that holds the political part of this together.

In The Broken Wings, Selma Karamy does not lose because she failed to make the better argument. There is no fair argument to win. The bishop controls the standard, benefits from the decision, and sits above appeal. His robe turns power into propriety.

The robe changes shape easily.

A university can define merit, sell access to the definition, then point to the credential as proof the definition worked. A platform can eat culture, own the route to the audience, generate substitutes for the people whose work fed it, set the price of visibility, and call itself neutral.

Creative communities can wear the robe too. A human-only practice can matter. Consent matters. Labor matters. Process can be part of the work. But pedigree can hide inside ethics. Familiarity becomes authenticity. Difficulty becomes virtue. Membership becomes merit.

I wore the robe whenever my intention outranked somebody else's experience. I wrote the rule, took the verdict, and made appeal expensive.

Hating the robe does not prevent me from putting it on.

That applies to founders, rebels, artists, scientists, AI critics, anti-capitalists, and anybody else who has correctly identified a cage and therefore starts believing they are incapable of building the next one.

Fuck a black box. Also, fuck a glass prison.

I do not want every person forced to publish their trauma, medical history, private messages, or inner life. People need protected interiors. Power and public claims need an open surface. If a system acts, show what authority it had and what happened afterward. If somebody makes a scientific claim, show the source and the check. If a work is sold on a claim about its process, tell the truth about what entered it.

The person can keep an interior. The work does not get to certify itself.

[Build energy.]

AI has thrown this problem into nearly every field at once.

For a long time, hard work and the route taken by the worker traveled together. A solved problem implied study. A polished illustration implied practice. Working code implied some contact with the machine underneath it.

Then the bundle split open.

A person can now produce expert-shaped work without traveling the expected road. That can be liberating as hell. Pedigree excluded people for reasons that had very little to do with what they could contribute: money, disability, geography, family obligation, language, neurotype, timing, and the thousand accidents that decide who gets a clean runway.

I feel that opening personally. I missed the official road into research. These tools gave me a route back toward the questions.

The opening did not arrive clean.

Generation got faster. Verification did not. The cost moved onto maintainers, teachers, editors, researchers, clients, and audiences. I call that review debt. Somebody pays it even when the invoice is hidden.

Creative workers pay another version. Their craft paid rent. It formed identity and community. When a client can ask for ten plausible images in an afternoon, the illustrator does not receive a philosophical lesson about technological change. They receive a lower rate or no call at all.

I have no patience for the people who answer that pain with, "Adapt." That is capitalist sadism in a futurist T-shirt.

The AI companies are full of shit when they swallow enormous portions of human culture, turn it into private capability, meter access to the result, and call the rental democratization.

The evangelists are full of shit when they say "just a tool" as though tools have no owner, labor effect, energy cost, training history, or politics.

The purists can be full of shit too. They are right about extraction and collapsing rates. Some still turn suffering into a credential. Hours become moral worth. Scarcity becomes authenticity. Every hybrid practice becomes fraud while all the software, assistants, stock libraries, samplers, fabrication, and invisible labor under supposedly pure production disappear.

Human-made does not guarantee humane.

And yes, I am using AI to help write this talk.

No reveal. No gotcha.

I gave it my essays, comments, research notes, personal history, and a lot of angry feedback when it produced prose that sounded like a tasteful museum plaque about my life. It helped me search, sort, challenge, and edit. I chose what stayed. I am the one standing here.

I will not call this human-written because that label would be convenient. It is AI-assisted and mine because I can explain why the claims are here, I accept responsibility for the result, and you can contradict me.

The machine cannot apologize for me. It cannot owe my family anything. It cannot absorb the consequence if I use confession to manipulate this room.

That part is mine.

[Shift to discovery. Curious, less angry.]

Today, Dmitry Rybin published an explicit graph that he says kills a cost conjecture in single-source unsplittable flow, associated with Dinitz, Garg, and Goemans. The problem had been open for roughly thirty years.

The proposed graph has a fractional flow of cost 58. Every unsplittable flow within the stated additive capacity violation of 15 costs at least 60. Rybin says he found it after pushing GPT-5.6 Pro through about five hours of search.

The graph still deserves independent reconstruction. Good. That is the point.

A universal conjecture says no counterexample exists. One graph can end it. The graph does not care whether it came from a famous lab, a graduate seminar, a private notebook, or a long session with a model. It survives the check or it does not.

This is where AI makes sense to me. Let it search a stupidly large space. Let it keep going after a person gets tired. Then make the object touch something that can say no.

The model's confidence means nothing. Rebuild the flow. Check the costs. Prove the bound. Publish the object and let people attack it.

Something happened here. The search boundary moved. The proof boundary did not.

Mathematics has a luxury art does not. A graph can kill a conjecture. A poem has no kernel.

A painting cannot compile into beauty. A song cannot fail because its grief went outside tolerance.

Good.

So the question for art is different. Can we build generative work without depending on a hidden inherited model trained across an undeclared cultural archive?

I think we can.

Start below the prompt. Build the instrument.

The artist chooses the primitives: points, curves, glyphs, meshes, particles, colors, marks, materials, sounds. Then the laws: grammars, collision rules, growth, cellular systems, fluid approximations, shaders, custom physics.

Variation can come from a hand gesture, a drawing, a microphone, local weather, movement, a plant, a machine, electrical noise in the room, or material a collaborator chose to contribute.

Search can be evolutionary, procedural, or learned inside the world the artist built. A small neural system can start from random weights and learn from self-authored material, licensed work, public-domain sources, or synthetic simulation.

The artist is not asking a hidden archive to imitate a finished surface. They are building the possibility space, entering it, and finding out what they made possible by accident.

The generator becomes part of the artwork.

When I think about Jacob's Ladder, Platoon, Dali, or Beksinski, I do not want a machine to copy the surface. I want to ask what reached me underneath it: dissonance, sacrifice, impossible space, the body as an empty shell, some thread still pulling toward wonder.

Then turn those relations into forces, light, geometry, timing, and material. Not "in the style of." A new instrument built around the pressure beneath the style.

We can record the process too. What entered? What stayed out? Which parts learned? Where did the person intervene? What compute was used? Which failed paths led to the final thing?

That record does not prove beauty or originality. It tells the truth about lineage.

Nothing comes from nowhere. Novelty is a relation that did not exist before, made through an ancestry we are willing to name.

[Build toward conclusion.]

The old world gatekept knowledge through pedigree. The new one is learning to gatekeep it through an invoice.

Metered tokens. Closed weights. Shifting plans. Cloud-only workflows. The surface is open as long as you keep paying the landlord of the surface.

Fuck that little pipe.

I am anti-capitalist enough to say fuck capitalism, technofeudalism, and autocracy. I am old enough to know a slogan is not architecture. I can say "stab it in the heart" all day and still wake up inside the same billing portal.

So the work gets boring and concrete.[Open the possibility. Less anger, more invitation.]

There is another thing I want to build, because criticism alone does not feed anybody.

Artists already work through references, moodboards, collages, shared folders, and chains of influence. What if the creative workspace kept a living source graph for the things a person actually brought into the room?

Not a plagiarism cop. Not a machine trying to calculate who owns twelve percent of a brushstroke.

A record. This photograph. That poster. This palette. That composition. The earlier artist's name, link, license, and preferred credit stay attached instead of disappearing the second their work enters somebody else's process.

If the final work earns money, the people in that declared chain could receive a share when the relationship is clear and everybody agreed to it.

The analogy in my head is a modlist that can assemble a world without pretending it authored every mod inside it. Coordination is real work. Coordination does not require erasure.

The platform also needs a place where people can settle differences without immediately turning one another into enemies. Show the process. Correct the record. Offer credit. Remove the source. Negotiate a license. Disagree. Maybe collaborate later.

I want a steward, not another thief standing between the artist and the audience.

The platform should not own the work, the identity, the social graph, or the relationship. If it charges, the fee should be visible and tied to something it actually did. People should be able to leave with their records and their community intact.

The same thing applies to every service platform I care about.

Its success should be measured partly by how much time it gives back.

Did the system save the user ten hours? Fine. Who received those ten hours?

If the person gets two and the company turns eight into margin, the system did not return time. It extracted a productivity gain and put liberation on the invoice.

I want a time dividend. The person gets to keep the hour for rest, family, study, art, or doing absolutely nothing. The platform does not get to call life "unused capacity" and sell it again.

Maybe we can all eat if we stop hoarding the food.

[Widen the frame.]

And I do not think we have to wait for the current system to grow a conscience.

A creative economy can be built beside it.

Not another marketplace with softer branding. A community-owned infrastructure where the chain exists to support the structure instead of siphoning nutrients out of it.

The source graph preserves lineage. The licensing layer preserves consent. The rendering tools help make the work. The epistemic engine keeps contribution, uncertainty, and dispute visible. The treasury routes value back through the artists, maintainers, moderators, teachers, compute providers, and people doing the care work that keeps a community alive.

Each layer feeds the next without becoming its landlord.

That is accountable-from-the-bottom-up infrastructure. The people affected by a rule can inspect it, argue with it, change it, leave it, and carry their work somewhere else.

It will not be pure. We are still human. We will compete, hoard, form cliques, protect our friends, punish our enemies, and call whatever benefits us ethical.

Good. Design for the person who actually exists.

Keep the receipt. Who supplied the source? Who agreed? Who worked? Who maintained the tool? Who received the money? What remains disputed? Who changed the rule, and who benefited?

The goal is not a universal score for human worth. It is a surface we can reopen when power and memory begin editing the story.

This could create domains that barely have names yet: source-bounded generative art, cooperative rendering, community-owned creative models, open research studios, shared fabrication, local learning networks, service platforms that return time instead of consuming it.

Maybe the old system gets dismantled by building enough exits that its worst assumptions stop being necessary for survival.

And if we believe any of the ethics we keep performing in public, then we have to start by being honest about who we are.

All of us.

Capable of building the table. Capable of hoarding everything on it.

Build for that person, and give the community a way to see when the steward starts becoming a thief.

[Bring the engine back. Firm. Less abstraction.]

This is not separate from the accountable engine. It is why I want one.

I keep wanting to call it a perfect platform for honest infrastructure.

Perfect is robe language.

I do not need a perfect system. I need one that can fail where other people can see the failure, keep the record intact, and let somebody affected reopen the case.

I say receipts over encryption. Let me be precise. Encryption protects a secret. We still need privacy. Opening the box cannot mean turning a person into glass.

But encryption does not tell us whether an authorized person lied, whether a rule moved after the fact, or whether a gate protects the public or the gatekeeper. A receipt preserves what happened. Verification checks the claim. Appeal gives the person living under the decision somewhere to stand.

The door can be open while the action remains bounded.

A bank cannot let everybody rewrite a ledger. An archive cannot let every visitor replace the record. Fine. Bound the action.

But why can't everybody enter the process? Inspect the rule. Submit a claim. Run a public check. Challenge a refusal. Contribute a bounded fix. See the evidence that decided the case.

Open access is not root access.

Propriety, credentials, forms, and queues were often substitutes for verification. Then they became a class system with stationery.

And yes, we will fuck this up.

I am not trying to build for purified people. I know what I can do when truth becomes expensive. The system has to assume that builders, stewards, users, and critics will sometimes lie, hoard, protect friends, punish enemies, and edit the story.

So let the mistake leave a mark.

This was claimed. This action was taken. This person was affected. This is what happened afterward. This part is disputed. This repair was attempted. This rule changed. Here is who benefited.

If we are fucked up, let us fuck up in the open.

Not as spectacle. Not as absolution. As a record that cannot quietly disappear.

I think most of us know some version of the red button. Sometimes it points inward. Sometimes at the person we do not want to answer to. Sometimes at the evidence.

I have known more than one of those buttons.

We can crumble. We can breathe and maintain. We can go outside and hurt somebody else because facing the truth feels worse in the moment.

No system chooses for us. Agency is still ours.

We can walk the path that calls us without turning somebody else into the road.

But a system can stop arranging every incentive around denial. A community can make repair cheaper than lying, cooperation easier than hoarding, and criticism something we learn from instead of a death sentence or a marketing asset.

We have been eating our own lack of accountability and feeding it to the next generation.

Hope is not enough.

Break the wall where it blocks entry. Build the boundary where an action can hurt someone. Put the receipt where the story can be rewritten. Build the foundation wide enough for everybody to stand on.

We do not exist to stand on anyone else.

Nobody should have to become the floor.

I am a living, breathing reaction

I am not a clean origin point. Nobody is.

I am a reaction to my family, my peers, the women I loved and hurt, the men I copied, the rooms I survived, the institutions I distrusted, the music that taught me how to posture, the art that taught me how to ache, the drugs I used to leave myself, and the code that let me build another world.

Environment enters. I transform it. Consequence leaves.

That is not an acquittal. A reaction still has products. People still breathe what comes out of the chamber.

It is also why I think AI can become what sampling became for music.

Sampling did not prove musicians ran out of ideas. It proved culture was already recursive. A drum break could cross a decade, a machine, a neighborhood, and a different grief, then become something that did not exist before.

The ugliest part was not transformation. It was the ownership system around the masters. The labels. The clearance. The distribution choke points. People making themselves indispensable by standing between artists and one another.

AI is rebuilding the record label around the sampler.

I want the model to become an instrument. I want a source graph that keeps lineage visible. I want artists to name what entered, what was licensed, what was refused, what was transformed, and who should receive credit or an agreed share. I want the community to own the tool and the relationship after the introduction.

Fuck the label. Keep the lineage.

And every domain has record labels. Journals. Credentials. Banks. Clouds. Bureaucracies. Coordination turns into ownership, then ownership calls itself order.

I know the language I inherited for defiance. The music taught me lines like, "ima fuck yo bitch." I understand the voltage. It says: I can enter your world, take what you call yours, and make your authority look weak.

But a woman is not proof that I defeated another man.

If I am trying to dismantle ownership, I cannot keep turning a woman into property every time I want to sound powerful.

So I want to keep the defiance and kill the possession.

Fuck the monopoly, not the woman. Fuck the gate, not the person trapped beside it. Fuck the label, the landlord, the lie, and the little pipe they expect everybody else to drink from.

Come get me means inspect the work. Contradict me. Rebuild it. Fork it. Show me the receipt. I do not need to threaten a body to stand behind an idea.

My principle is fertile. Let it impregnate the culture, not possess a person. Breed it. Fork it. Mutate it. Give birth to something better than me.

I may still use the old language. Knowing better has never guaranteed I behave better.

But I want to learn to call women women.

I want to see myself as a man.

Not domination. Provision without possession. Protection without control. Strength that can hear no. The ability to remain present after the explanation stops working.

I want to provide something good.

That does not make me good.

Sincerity is not a receipt.

The work has to survive contact with another person's reality. It has to be useful without requiring faith in me. It has to preserve an exit when I become the obstacle.

I am a living, breathing reaction.

That means I carry what came before me.

It also means the reaction is still happening. Local-first tools. Smaller models made more capable through good scaffolding and verification. Portable histories. Open protocols. Community-owned compute. Cooperative licensing. Labor agreements that return productivity gains to workers. The right to run offline. The right to inspect, repair, fork, and leave.

Receipts instead of badges.

A badge says, "Trust me, I belong here." A receipt gives you something to walk.

The earth belongs inside the receipt too. The cloud is somebody else's building, sitting on somebody's grid, drawing from somebody's watershed. If the creative future depends on invisible sacrifice zones, the cage did not disappear. It moved.

[Slow down.]

I keep returning to the Mad-Happy Scientist because I think a lot of people have one.

Maybe yours was an artist, astronaut, mechanic, naturalist, teacher, writer, musician, healer, or the kid who took apart every broken object in the house to see whether failure had a shape.

Then life happened. Some people jumped through the hoops. Some tripped. Some were pushed out. Some turned left. Some found a job that paid the rent and slowly separated them from the part of themselves that asked questions for no reason besides wonder.

I treated the missed road into formal research as a verdict. I let pedigree become metaphysics.

Then I resented the institution for a cage I was helping maintain.

That resentment fed grandiosity. Every project had to prove everything. Every good week had to become an empire. I kept trying to swallow the sun and acting surprised when it burned.

Grandiosity and shame are close relatives. One says I deserve the keys to the city. The other says I am too corrupt to enter. Both keep the whole world organized around me.

Ordinary accountable work is smaller.

One test that can fail. One claim another person can inspect. One artist who names the lineage. One apology made without controlling the response. One boundary accepted even though it hurts. One door left open behind you.

I still want to pick the lock. I probably always will.

The change is that I do not want to pick it only for myself.

I want to open the cage for everybody, throw away the key, and let the walls rot into soil.

That should make you suspicious. People promising universal liberation have worn a lot of robes.

So contradict me. Inspect the system. Reject the doctrine. Fork the tool. Leave the platform. Tell me the argument breaks. Nothing I build gets authority from the fact that I hate authority.

I used to want a killing blow against my ego. Even that can be theater. There is no terminal command for the self. Self-destruction removes the person who owes the work.

The apology has to be less cinematic. Tell the truth before exposure forces it. Correct the record. Pay the cost you moved. Accept another person's boundary. Listen without editing their account. Do not demand forgiveness as the prize for finally admitting what happened.

Then continue, because repair needs somebody alive to do it.

[Final rise. Do not rush.]

AI is here. Hypocrisy is here too.

Let go of the old ethics that ask people to die proving they were pure. Keep the ethics that protect agency, consent, livelihood, relation, accountability, and the earth.

Aim the machinery somewhere useful.

Toward discovery. Toward local capability. Toward workers sharing the gain. Toward art with an honest lineage. Toward science that keeps the receipt. Toward communities that can refuse. Toward tools we can inspect, repair, fork, and leave.

I do not know how to solve the human condition. I do not know shit, and I am trying to stop turning that uncertainty into paralysis one day and authority the next.

I know the cage was built. Built things can be taken apart.

I know a black box can be opened without turning a person into glass.

I know a theorem can meet a kernel and a poem can stay free.

I know a confession is not a receipt. It can still be the first honest entry in a record that has been lying for a long time.

The Mad-Happy Scientist is still in there. Pissed off, tired, and waiting for me to stop asking whether I deserve to begin.

So get out of the mud. Do not pretend it is imaginary. Do not climb onto somebody else's back.

Get out, turn around, and put something down that gives the next person traction.

Pick the lock for everyone.

Let the walls rot.

Make room for something we could not imagine yesterday.

[Drop the rise. Speak plainly.]

I need to end with something less heroic.

I believe this hard enough that I am afraid of failing it.

I do not trust myself to live by every word I just said for the rest of my life. I know my patterns. I know what illness, shame, appetite, exhaustion, and fear can do. I still doubt myself. I am not going to clean that doubt out of the ending.

Mental health matters for everybody. Find a way back to yourself that actually works for you. Faith. Meditation. Therapy. Medication. Curiosity. Reflection. Art. Community. Maybe several of them. Let somebody help hold the door open when you cannot.

I should be honest about my route. I do not do well with one-on-one therapy. I still prefer self-medication more often than I should. That is not advice. It is part of the record.

Sometimes refusing an institution is self-knowledge. Sometimes it is avoidance wearing self-determination as a costume. A path your spirit did not choose can become another cage. A self-chosen path can still walk you straight into a wall.

Help is not surrender. Autonomy is not immunity. I have to ask whether the route makes me more present, less destructive, and more able to answer for what I do.

I said I like to sit in my pain. The honest answer is that I do a bit of both.

Sometimes I sit beside it. Sometimes I sit inside it and call the punishment reflection. I have made a home there before. I know the furniture.

I own that.

I am also beginning to learn how to love myself again.

I believe everybody deserves redemption. Not erasure. Not automatic forgiveness. Redemption.

Sometimes the bridge over the canyon fails and you go tumbling down. You do not wake up back on the road. You still have to get up and make the slow march out of the canyon.

Maybe you crawl for part of it. Maybe somebody walks beside you.

But you move.

Otherwise you are just a memory of yesterday.

[Stay plain. This is not a savior speech.]

I want to swallow the darkness and release light in the form of spiritual and mental sincerity.

I think about The Green Mile. The fantasy of taking pain out of somebody, holding it long enough to change its direction, and leaving something gentler behind.

I understand why that image stays with me. I spent a lot of my life taking pain in, mixing it with my own, and throwing it back into the room in a worse shape.

I do not want martyrdom to become another escape. Nobody should have to absorb the whole world's pain until they disappear. The gift has to be a thought, a tool, a structure, a place where the next person does not have to carry the same weight alone.

So, to the young people, the young men and women, and anybody else listening: do not take me as an example of how to behave.

I repeated the same mistakes as people before me. I made the same excuses and found better language for making them. I took what I received from my father, my mother, my stepfather, my brother, my peers, my exes, and every room that taught me how to survive. I internalized it, distorted it, and sometimes handed it to somebody else.

My behavior reflects that environment. It is not the whole of my intention. It is still my behavior.

Environment is the medium, not an acquittal. It shapes a person, an animal, an idea, a song, a wound, a criticism. That is also why transformation is possible. Change the conditions. Keep the record. Make another choice. Something else can grow.

Until somebody dies, another move remains. Even after a life ends, the impact keeps moving. Harm can stay harm and still teach somebody where the cliff is.

I believe in everybody. Even people who cannot believe in themselves.

I believe in you.

I am trying to believe in me.

I am not announcing an exit. I am here. I stand behind the idea. I am putting it down because anything I want to survive a mood has to leave my head.

Record it. Archive it. Let people argue with it. Then turn it toward something material. Healing. Learning. Art. Discovery. Food. Rent. Time. People getting paid enough to care for the people they love.

Everybody is only a few decisions, a few losses, or one missing hand away from the person beside them they judge.

Desperation is not a character test.

The middleman can be a regular man too. A steward at the table instead of the owner of it. We can make the thing, share the gain, and leave enough behind for somebody else to begin.

I said a sincere intention to do good cannot be tarnished. I know what I mean. I also have to apply the same rule here that I apply everywhere else.

Sincerity is not a receipt.

A good intention can be contradicted by behavior. It can become another robe. The gift begins when sincerity survives contact with somebody else's reality and becomes something they can use without having to trust me.

That is what I want to leave behind.

I cannot promise perfect behavior. That would be another lie wearing a promise.

I can promise that I stand by the conviction. I can build a thought and a structure today that I will not tear down tomorrow to protect my ego. I can leave something other people can inspect, criticize, use, and carry farther than I can.

I want to build it for the people who endure, for the people we lost, and for the people who may not know whether they can endure what comes next.

Not a monument.

A handrail.

A place to put some weight down.

Maybe I fail this conviction. Maybe I contradict it. Maybe one of you has to remind me what I said.

The structure should survive that.

It should survive me.
