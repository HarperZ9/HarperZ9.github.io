Inequality jumped 4 points

The headline is printed in dots sized by the tone of Hugh Ferriss's 1922 drawing of the largest building the zoning law allowed. A room, drawn by its rules.

That is what the world average of the Gini index does between 2023 and 2024, in World Bank data. It is a real number, computed correctly. Decide whether you believe the headline before you look closer.

The world did not change. The room did.

The trick has a name in each field: survivorship, selection, a composition effect. 39 countries, 28 of them in Europe and Central Asia, where inequality runs low (their 2023 average was 32.3), had no 2024 figure yet; the 26 that did lean toward Latin America, where inequality is higher.

The same average is honest when the sample is fixed or shown. Polls, price indexes and census tables all average over whoever answered; they earn trust by saying who that was. An average can pull a pattern out of noise, or hang a false one on it.1
