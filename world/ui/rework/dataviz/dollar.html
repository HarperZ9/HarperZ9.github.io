The title is the data. Read it in order: each column of dots is one year, 1913 at the first letter and 2024 at the last. A dot's area is what a 1913 dollar still buys that year.

A dollar in 1913 bought a basket of things. Every year since, prices moved. Here is the dollar as a sheet that thins as it buys less, rippling with each year's inflation.

The deflator is a rope

Drawn in nominal terms, a century of prices looks like a cliff, and that picture has sold gold, crypto and fear for decades. The same index, used honestly, is what turns a 1950 wage into today's money, so a pension, a contract or a history book can compare like with like. It can pull a comparison out of the pit, or hang it.1
