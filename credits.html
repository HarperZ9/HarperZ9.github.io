Archival plates

The exhibit's pictures, film and sound are public domain, CC0 or CC BY, each with its source: the exhibit's credits. The shell's plates are public domain scans from open archives, among them Muybridge's cockatoo, Animal Locomotion plate 758 (National Gallery of Art, CC0), Goya's Nobody knows himself (The Metropolitan Museum of Art, CC0) and Doré's bust of Münchhausen (Wikimedia Commons, public domain). The home's dial is engraved from Regiomontanus's astrolabe of 1462 (The Metropolitan Museum of Art, CC0), and the Low Water loop is low tide from Heavenly Bodies, 1920 (Prelinger Archives, public domain); the dial's credits. Two of the drawn link-preview covers are redrawn from colour plates: the wheel on hub pages from Chevreul's first chromatic circle, 1864 (Science History Institute, on Wikimedia Commons, public domain), and the banded ball on tool pages from Runge's colour sphere, 1810 (Wikimedia Commons, public domain). Neither plate is reproduced; each gave the drawing its structure. The covers are drawn as cut paper in flat colour with cast shadows, a manner learned from the Eames House of Cards, Giovanni Pintori's Olivetti posters and the books of Bruno Munari and Enzo Mari; none of that work is reproduced.
